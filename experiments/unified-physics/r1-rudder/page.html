What is the model and what is a stand-in

From the model

  • Hull, keel and rudder side force, turning, leeway and the stern swinging out: every section trades sideways momentum with slices of water it slides over. Solid arrows.
  • Water the hull carries round in a turn (why a tack costs speed), not reduced.
  • Rudder stall: one lagged attachment number per section, same constants for every fin. The rudder light goes amber, then red.
  • Heel from sail force, stability and crew weight; friction drag.
  • The two boats differ only in their numbers: section shapes, weight, stability, sail plan.

Stand-ins

  • Sails: a table computed beforehand for rigid shapes (small steady lattice, strips fading into a flat plate when stalled). No cloth, no flapping; a slack main or a jib let fly simply carries nothing. Dashed arrows.
  • Wave-making drag: Delft series table from each hull's dimensions.
  • Keel wash on the rudder: probably too strong.
  • Flat water, steady wind, no gusts except the button, no wind change with height.
  • Hull shapes are built from main dimensions, not from the lines plans. Folkboat stability and keel shape are estimates.

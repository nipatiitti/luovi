What is the model and what is a stand-in

From the model

  • Hull, keel and rudder side force, turning, leeway and the stern swinging out: every section trades sideways momentum with slices of water it slides over. Solid arrows.
  • Water the hull carries round in a turn (why a tack costs speed), not reduced.
  • Rudder stall: one lagged attachment number per section, same constants for every fin; the blade counts as a foil of its own from its hinge. Once it lets go it carries the flat-plate force and mostly brakes. The rudder light goes amber, then red.
  • Heel changes the hull: it rises, its under-water shape goes lopsided and turns the bow to windward, the rudder works about a leaning stock, and sail drive and hull drag pull on opposite sides.
  • Rudder sucking air: when the suction on the blade exceeds the depth of water over it, air comes down from the surface and that part of the blade loses grip. From astern the blade shows grey (out of the water), amber (aired) and purple (in solid water), with the share left in water; the “aired” light goes amber, then red.
  • Heel from sail force, stability and crew weight; friction drag.
  • The two boats differ only in their numbers: section shapes, weight, stability, sail plan.

Stand-ins

  • Sails: a table computed beforehand for rigid shapes (small steady lattice, strips fading into a flat plate when stalled). No cloth, no flapping; a slack main or a jib let fly simply carries nothing. Dashed arrows.
  • Wave-making drag: Delft series table from each hull's dimensions.
  • Keel wash on the rudder: probably too strong.
  • Air on the rudder: a simple head-of-water rule, not checked against measurements. Topsides are straight from the waterline to the deck edge.
  • The tiller is held by hand at the angle you set; a let-go, free-swinging tiller is in the model but not on this page.
  • Flat water, steady wind, no gusts except the button, no wind change with height.
  • The H-boat is from the class offsets and plans: hull sections, keel, deadwood, rudder, mast and sail plan, weight and stability (class-minimum boat with 300 kg of crew). Each hull section is still an ellipse, of the real waterline beam and immersed area; the mast stands upright where the raked rig's centre of area is. The Folkboat is built from main dimensions; its stability and keel shape are estimates.

# Side-force architecture of the hull chapter against yacht hydrodynamics literature

Research for issue #17, 2026-10-21. Question: how validated yacht models split hull, keel and rudder side force and over which drift and speed ranges; whether a vortex-lattice method (VLM) with a free-surface image is the right small-drift model for a fin-keel yacht and how accurate it is against tank data; whether the switch-versus-add question for lift and cross-flow drag is settled; which transient lift effects matter for tacking and waves; and which tank data could become a side-force benchmark for the H-boat class. Scope split with #15: that ticket owns large-drift coefficients and derivatives, this one owns the architecture and the small-drift regime.

The single most important architecture finding is the per-tick cost. A full matrix-vector product with the precomputed inverse is 360 000 multiply-adds at 600 panels, roughly a third of the hero boat's 1 ms on its own. Because the right-hand side is linear in a few kinematic quantities, the same answer comes from about 30 precomputed basis solutions per heel bin, which is 18 000 multiply-adds per tick (section 7). That also makes the per-strip corrections below (pressure relief, stall weight, Wagner lag) essentially free.

Observations that matter for the chapter:

- **The keel dominates, but the canoe body is not negligible.** For a beamy 30 ft hull the bare hull gave about 30 % of the side-force slope at 20 degrees heel [YA05 Fig. 6, read off the plot]. Slooff's Fig. 6.3.9 shows the side-force slope rising almost linearly with hull depth to keel span D_h/b_k through carry-over [SL15 §6.3]. The H-boat's D_h/b_k is about 0.79 (canoe body plus stub 0.553 m to the keel root at VL8, keel span 0.70 m [XF]), high, so carry-over is a large share.
- **The rudder must sit in the keel's downwash.** Slooff found the keel downwash and rudder forces "significant" when matching the K1 to K9 slopes [SL15 App. E, after Fig. E.2]. Every model above includes it.
- **The hull yaw moment is a separate, non-lifting term** in Slooff (App. G) and in the extended keel method (Munk moment). A thin flat hull lattice does not produce it.
- **Nothing read validates beyond about 10 degrees of leeway, below Fn 0.25, or at all at zero speed.** The cross-flow regime and the blend therefore rest on #15.

Reading of the evidence: a lifting-surface method on hull, keel and rudder is the method class every yacht manoeuvring model uses at small drift, and its accuracy is about 5 to 15 % in the linear range when (i) the rudder is included in the keel wake, (ii) the canoe body is given circulation (the [SY95] NCAC case shows what happens without it) and (iii) the zero-leeway heel effect is handled.

### 2.2 Known gaps of a thin VLM with a rigid-wall image

1. **Free-surface pressure relief.** The rigid-wall (reflection) image over-predicts side force and under-predicts induced drag; tank data suggest an effective span near the actual draft rather than twice it (Gerritsma 1971; Beukelman and Keuning 1975; Marchaj, all as cited in [SL15 §6.3]). Section 3 gives the correction.
2. **Anti-image is wrong too.** It forces Γ to zero at the surface, which implies trailing vorticity in a nearly flat free surface and "leads to a significant underestimation of the total side force" [SL15 §6.3]. The chapter's blend from rigid-wall to anti-image with Fn is therefore a blend between two models the most detailed source rejects.
3. **Heel-induced side force at zero leeway.** "The wetted part of a heeled hull will act as an asymmetric foil but since any thickness effects have been omitted this is not captured" [AS19 §3.1]. Section 4.
4. **Low-aspect-ratio non-linear lift.** Side-edge separation adds lift that peaks near A = 1 and almost doubles maximum lift there [SL15 §5.15, Fig. 5.15.17]. The H-boat keel, geometric A_k 0.55 (effective about 1.1), is in that regime. A VLM is linear; the cross-flow term in an additive model supplies the missing sin² part (section 5).
5. **No stall** (section 5).
6. **Keel wake on the rudder.** A trailing wake from the hull or keel pierces the rudder lattice and makes the solve unstable; [AS19 §3.1] forces the hull wake to trail at least 1 degree until clear of the rudder and uses a mollified vortex core [AS19 eq. 8, p_m = 3, c_m = ln 2].

## 3. The pressure-relief model in detail

Source: [SL15 §6.3 and App. E]. Slooff calls it heuristic; κ is "the author's impression".

### 3.1 Physics

The equal-pressure condition at the free surface means the local side force at the waterline must be zero at every Froude number, although the circulation there need not be. Slooff models the streamline elevation under the surface as a travelling-wave disturbance that decays exponentially with depth, with the chord as the length scale and a factor κ > 1 because the disturbance is short-crested [SL15 eq. E.8, E.13a, E.13b]:

Z_h(x, z) = Z_h(x, 0) · e^(2πκ z / c), z ≤ 0, **κ ≈ 1.5**.

Substituting into the pressure jump and using Γ = (1/2V)∮(V₋² − V₊²)dx gives the side force per unit span [SL15 eq. 6.3.7, E.28]:

**S(z) = ρV · [Γ(z) − Γ(0) · e^(2πκ z / c)]**

The first term is classical Kutta-Joukowski; the second is the relief. A waterline-elevation term (Fig. 6.3.2, S_w) is proportional to Fr⁴ and is neglected [SL15 eq. E.29b].

### 3.2 General form for a tapered, swept fin on a hull

- Tapered fin: the root chord c_R governs; for a straight taper the exponent is πκA_k(1 + TR)z/b, which is the same as 2πκz/c_R, and the relief at depth z scales with the local chord [SL15 eq. E.38 to E.40]:
  S(z) = ρV · [Γ(z) − Γ_R · (c(z)/c_R) · e^(2πκ z / (c_R cos Λ))]
- Sweep: z/b becomes z/(b cos Λ) [SL15 eq. E.41].
- Hull: the keel's root circulation Γ_R is carried at constant strength from the keel root up to the waterline, and the relief over the hull depth uses the keel's root chord c_R and TR = 1 [SL15 eq. E.54]. Over the fin itself z is measured from the water surface.
- Heel: replace κ by κ cos φ; "negligible for most if not all practical purposes" [SL15 App. E, Effects of Heel].
- Circulation and downwash are **not** relieved. The downwash behind the keel uses the lift "without free surface effect" [SL15 eq. F.9], and the induced drag factor rises by 1/F_FS [SL15 eq. E.64].

Closed forms for an elliptic loading, used here only to size the effect:

- Surface-piercing fin, zero sweep [SL15 eq. 6.3.10]: F_FS = 1 − (2/(π²κA_k))·(1 − e^(−2πκA_k)), applied to C_S0 computed with aspect ratio 2A_k. Values for κ = 1.5: A_k 0.5 → 0.73, 1 → 0.87, 2 → 0.93, 5 → 0.97.
- Fin on a hull [SL15 eq. E.56 to E.61]: F_FS = [1 + (4/π)F_R·(D_h/b − F1.1 − F2.1 − F1.2)] / [1 + (4/π)F_R·D_h/b], with F_R = 1 for elliptic loading. As printed, F1.2 has (1 + D_h/b) in its exponent; integrating E.54 directly gives D_h/b. The difference is under 0.01 in F_FS for the H-boat.

### 3.3 Where it enters a VLM

The VLM solves Γ with the rigid-wall image as now. The relief is a post-processing of the force on each spanwise strip j at depth z_j below the waterline at the current heel:

F_j = ρ · (V × l_j) · [Γ_j − Γ_wl · w_j], with w_j = (c_j / c_wl) · e^(2πκ z_j / (c_wl cos Λ_j))

where Γ_wl is the strip circulation at the waterline end of the surface (for a fin keel continued through the hull: the carried-over root circulation) and c_wl the chord of the lift-generating fin at its root. The weights w_j are geometry only, so they are computed per heel bin at load. Per tick this is one multiply-add per strip. In a strip (lifting-line) calculation the same weight multiplies the local Γ(0).

Two choices Slooff does not settle and #16 must: which chord governs for a `hullBody` lifting surface with no fin (the Joutsen), and whether κ should vary with Fn for the v2 maxi. Slooff states the model "does not model the effects of the length of the hull or Froude number" [SL15 App. E].

So for the H-boat the rigid-wall image over-predicts side-force slope by about 15 % (range 11 to 24 % over κ 2 to 1). The relief e-folds over c_R/(2πκ) = 0.17 m: the weight is 0.55 at 0.1 m depth, 0.17 at 0.3 m and 0.04 at the keel root, so almost all of it acts on the carried-over hull circulation. It also lowers the centre of lateral resistance, which changes the heeling arm.

Heel: Slooff's analysis of an "average" cruiser shows the loss of side-force slope with heel is dominated by cos² φ, with effective-span and relief changes small (Fig. 6.7.4) [SL15 §6.7.2]. In a body-fixed VLM, cos² φ comes from the kinematics (the keel sees v·cos φ and its lift is projected by cos φ). That is why heel bins can be coarse.

For the architecture: the ORC model shows the effect follows from heeled-hull geometry (fore and aft buoyancy shift is an effective yaw and camber of the heeled body). A lattice built per heel bin on the mid-surface of the heeled canoe body (midpoints between port and starboard surfaces on each heeled waterline) carries that camber and yaw by construction, at no runtime cost, and works the same for a `hullBody` lifting surface. That is the "geometry before coefficients" option. It is untested: the DSYHS heeled zero-leeway intercepts are the check, and the cited λ0 regression is the fallback.

## 5. Switch versus add (question 3)

**Settled in the literature: add.**

- Obokata (1987) and Yoshimura et al. (2009) sum a lift term that vanishes at 90 degrees and at zero forward speed by its own form with a cross-flow term at every angle [XF §5].
- Angelou and Spyrou decompose the hull reaction into "lifting forces, cross-flow effects, ideal fluid forces and forward resistance" and sum them, with Söding's cross-flow and Hoerner coefficients [AS19 §4.1; AS21 §3].
- Low-aspect-ratio wing theory is additive by construction: potential lift plus a separated cross-flow term in sin² α. Slooff's side-edge separation lift, largest near A = 1, is that term [SL15 §5.15, Fig. 5.15.17].

**The stall angle depends on aspect ratio.** Slooff's surface-piercing fin curves (Fig. 6.3.5, qualitative) peak near 14 degrees for A_k = ∞, about 16 for 5, about 20 for 2, about 25 for 1 and beyond 30 for 0.5 [SL15 §6.3]. Klaka's design rudder data peak at α 23 degrees with C_L 1.28 and C_N 1.38, and C_L can reach 1.5 under rapid deflection [KL20 App. 1]. Angelou and Spyrou cap rudder deflection at 15 degrees to stay attached [AS21 §3]. For the H-boat: keel A_k 0.55 keeps lifting beyond 30 degrees; rudder A 2.21 stalls near 20 degrees. A single 15 to 20 degree handover would cut the keel's lift far too early.

**Speed.** Forces from both terms scale with V², so the zero-speed limit is already right without a cut-off; what goes wrong at low speed is the inflow angle, which the angle weight handles. At pure sway (u = 0) the local angle is 90 degrees and the lift weight is zero by angle, not by speed.

**Recommended form for #16:**

F = Σ_surfaces w_s(α_s, A_s) · F_VLM,s + F_crossflow

where α_s is the local inflow angle of surface s in its own plane including rotation (v + r·x − p·z at its centre), w_s = 1 below a stall onset α_1(A_s) and falls smoothly to 0 by α_2(A_s), with α_1 and α_2 as cited or estimated per-surface values in the definition, and F_crossflow is #15's sectional integral, whose v_n must include p·z on the appendage strips so that roll at zero speed is damped. The weight is applied to the strip forces after the solve, which keeps the precomputed inverse. Stalling a surface this way leaves its downwash on the next surface in place, a known approximation.

## 7. Per-tick cost and the inverse

Estimated multiply-adds per tick, assuming about 1 ns each in typed-array JavaScript (an estimate, not a measurement):

| Panels | Full mat-vec with inverse | Superposition of 30 basis solutions |
| ------ | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 300    | 90 000 (≈ 0.09 ms)        | 9 000                               |
| 600    | 360 000 (≈ 0.36 ms)       | 18 000                              |
| 800    | 640 000 (≈ 0.64 ms)       | 24 000                              |

The no-penetration right-hand side on panel i is n_i·(V + ω × r_i + u_orb(r_i)). With a body-fixed lattice per heel bin it is linear in the six rigid-body velocities. For the rudder, rotating the normal n(δ) = cos δ·n0 + sin δ·t0 makes it a cos δ and sin δ combination of two more sets of six. A linear orbital field (value plus gradient at the hull centre) adds twelve. Precomputing the solution for each basis at load turns the solve into a weighted sum of about 30 vectors. Pressure relief, stall weights and Wagner states then act on per-surface or per-strip sums. Both the precompute and the sum use only add, multiply, divide and sqrt, so the determinism argument of #14 is unchanged; the Wagner and stall weights need `core`'s exp.

This also shows what the steady VLM is: per heel bin, a geometry-derived linear derivative set with interactions, plus a strip distribution. Its value over a regression is that the derivatives come from the hull's own geometry and the strip distribution supports relief, stall and lag.

## 9. Consequences for other tickets

### (a) Ship definition contract (#14, decided)

Fits in v1 if #16 defines the "VLM settings" block to hold it, since that block's shape is #16's to set:

- Free-surface rule: `rigidWallImage` plus `pressureRelief: { kappa }` with κ `{ value: 1.5, src: Slooff 2015 App. E }` (an estimate in the source itself) replacing "image rule by Froude number".
- Per lifting surface: stall onset and end angles (or the constants of an aspect-ratio law), cited or estimated.
- Unsteady filter coefficients (Jones Wagner and Küssner), cited, if they are treated as data rather than physics.
- Hull treatment choice for heel-induced side force, and the λ0 coefficient if the fallback is used.

Semantic clarifications to v1 (no schema change, but they change what values mean):

- Roll and yaw damping in the hydro block are the **non-lifting remainder**; the lift part comes from the VLM.
- Linear manoeuvring derivatives are not a separate force term in an additive architecture with a VLM; they are an output (as [XF §3.5] proposed) or, for a hull with no VLM, a fallback.

A v2 would need: a Froude-dependent relief for planing hulls, radiation state-space coefficients if memory effects are adopted, and a dynamic-stall lag constant.

### (b) VLM, heel bins, inverse and blend (#16)

The decision table at the top. In short: rigid-wall image with per-strip pressure relief; fin keel continued through the hull to the waterline, hull Munk moment separate, `hullBody` lattices on the heeled mid-surface; superposition of basis solutions; rudder by normal rotation; body-fixed prescribed wake with a vortex core; 10-degree heel bins; a reversed lattice for sternway; additive blend with per-surface, aspect-ratio-dependent stall weights; VLM owns induced drag; Wagner and Küssner lag per surface.

### (c) Ship state contract (#9)

- The Wagner and Küssner filter states (two per lifting surface each, so about 12 doubles for the H-boat) are integrator state and must be in the state array for replay and server determinism.
- Per-surface force, local angle and stall weight are derived and recomputable; if a renderer or UI wants them (a rudder-stall indicator), they go out as derived outputs, not state.
- If radiation memory is adopted later, its states join the array too.

### (d) Benchmarks

Add a DSYHS side-force method benchmark (section 8) next to the H-boat polar. Its numeric target needs the spreadsheets: proposed form "side-force slope within 10 % and zero-side-force leeway within 0.5 degrees at 0, 10, 20, 30 degrees heel" for each baked DSYHS model, to be confirmed against the data.

## 10. Design doc claims that should change

In `docs/intention/02-hull.md`, Side force:

1. "mirrored at the free surface (rigid-wall image at low Fn, anti-image at high Fn, blended between)": the source that analyses both rejects both; use a rigid-wall image plus pressure relief [SL15 §6.3, App. E].
2. "This gives keel–rudder upwash, heel-induced side force and rudder behaviour in the keel wake, which no regression captures": the Keuning and Verwerft regression and the ORC model capture keel downwash and heel-induced leeway [DAY17, ORC23]; a thin VLM does not capture heel-induced side force [AS19 §3.1] unless the hull lattice carries the heeled geometry.
3. "Beyond about 15–20° drift, or at any speed below ~0.3 kn, lift theory is meaningless": stall depends on aspect ratio (H-boat keel beyond 30 degrees), and no speed threshold is needed.
4. "Lift and cross-flow drag are blended by drift angle and speed so exactly one of them carries the side force at any moment" (Force modules): the literature adds them.
5. "plus linear manoeuvring derivatives (Y_v, N_v, Y_r, N_r)" in the cross-flow regime: with a VLM these duplicate its lift.
6. Rigid body: "roll, pitch and yaw damping terms" should say non-lifting, since the VLM supplies the lift part.
7. Resistance: "Budget per calibrated hull: well under a day on a workstation" is optimistic for planing hulls [LL17].

## Open questions

1. **Hull-lattice heel camber versus the λ0 regression** for zero-leeway heel force (section 4): the geometric option is untested and needs the DSYHS data.
2. **Pressure-relief length scale for a `hullBody` surface** (Joutsen) and any Froude dependence for the v2 maxi: Slooff gives neither.
3. **Wave drag due to side force**: 10 to 35 % of induced resistance at Fn 0.35, more for very low aspect-ratio keels such as the H-boat's [SL15 §6.5, App. J]. Neither the VLM nor the upright regression has it; a K_w·C_S² term is available in [SL15 App. J] if wanted.
4. **Units of λ0** in the Keuning and Verwerft regression: the transcription in [DAY17] does not state them; obtaining the 2009 paper settles it.
5. **The DSYHS spreadsheets** could not be downloaded here; the test matrix and the models with keel and rudder must be confirmed before the benchmark target is fixed.
6. **Radiation memory** in waves: needed for the broach and course-keeping cases, outside this ticket.
7. **Sign of the heel-induced force** in [YA05]: the paper does not state the heel direction relative to its side-force axis.

# Breaking cutoff: the shortest wave that whitecaps

Research for issue #42, 2026-10-24. Question: the shortest wavelength (or slowest phase speed) of a wind wave that breaks with air entrainment and so makes a whitecap, as a cited value for the **breaking cutoff** that Foam rework design (#40) put on the Jacobian channels; whether the boundary is sharp; and what it does to the breaking-weighted divergence variance and the solved breaking threshold on the build's three reference seas (`sim-ocean` `breaking.ts` on main at 6c95bd5). Facts and options only; the decision belongs to a later ticket.

Conversions: a breaking front speed c maps to a wavelength by the deep-water relation $L=2\pi c^2/g$ when c is taken as the phase speed, as [SM15] and [DLM17] do. [MM02] instead takes the front speed as 0.8 times the phase speed, which lengthens the wavelength by $1/0.8^2=1.56$. Both conventions appear below; every wavelength says which.

### 1.2 Dependence on wind and wave age

- **Fixed speed.** Phillips's whitecap threshold is on the energy release per unit crest length, $\epsilon_T=bc_T^5/g$, so $c_T$ is a constant for constant b, and his whitecap crest length and passage rate then grow as $u__^3$ [P85 eq. 6.11 to 6.13]. [SM15] and [DLM17] use a fixed 2 m/s across wave ages $c_m/u__$ of 16 to 150.
- **Through breaking strength.** b is not constant: it rises with slope, $b=0.4(S-0.08)^{5/2}$ [DLM17 §2.1, citing Romero et al. 2012], so a fixed $\epsilon_T$ would put $c_T$ lower in steeper seas (an inference from [P85] and [DLM17]; no source writes it).
- **Relative to the peak.** Visible breakers reach down to about 0.1 $c_p$ in developing seas, and the most frequent visible breaker moves from 0.4 to 0.2 $c_p$ with development [GBG08 abstract]. On the reference seas 0.1 $c_p$ is 0.70, 1.15 and 1.70 m/s, wavelengths 0.31, 0.84 and 1.85 m [M].
- **Wave age and wind.** Microbreakers carry a larger share of breaking dissipation in old, light-wind seas than in young seas in strong winds [SM15 §3]; for old seas "the predominant breaking scale transitions to the shortest whitecaps and non air-entraining microbreakers" [BM16 §1]. Aerated small breakers appear and vanish with the overall mean-square slope, and large whitecaps "replace, rather than simply add to" small ones as breaking strengthens [STG14 §4a].
- **Wind drift.** The surface drift q lowers the highest wave before breaking to $\zeta_{max}=\tfrac{c^2}{2g}(1-q/c)^2$ [BP74 abstract], so short waves whose speed approaches the drift break at small amplitude; drift grows with wind. The abstract gives no air entrainment criterion.
- **Surface tension and surfactants.** The 14 cm floor comes from the clean-water Bond number [DPM15]; surfactants change spilling breakers and suppress the capillary ripples (Liu and Duncan, second-hand via [DPM15 §5]). No source gives a correction for sea water.

No source gives $c_T$ or a cutoff wavelength as a function of $U_{10}$.

## 2. Sharp boundary or transition band

### 2.1 What the sources say about its shape

- **A continuum of breaking types.** Phillips describes "a range of scales from longer, vigorous events ... producing whitecapping, air entrainment and a trail of foam, through smaller, more transient events with splashing and a few air bubbles to much smaller breaking occurrences, microscale breaking" [P85 §2]. Melville: "This lack of a universal visual feature identifying breaking" [M96 p. 280].
- **In simulation.** The spilling to plunging boundary near Bo of 100 "is a smooth transition", with "highly aerated spilling breakers" between [DPM15 §3.2.4]; a small change in surface tension at slope 0.45 turns a bubble-free spiller (Bo = 25) into a bubbly one (Bo = 70) [DPM15 §3.1.4]. The boundary there depends on slope as well as scale, $\epsilon_B=K_B(1+Bo)^{-1/3}$.
- **In the field.** "The distinction between whitecaps and microbreakers is not straightforward"; small aerated breakers missed by a ship camera were seen from a buoy camera, "a broadband effect based more on the overall wave steepness" [STG14 §4a]. Visible Λ(c) peaks at 1 to 4 m/s and rolls off below [KM10]; [DLM17] place the visible peak near 2 m/s with roll-off for slower speeds.
- **Its width.** Read from the sources rather than given by them: [DLM17]'s "typically below 1 to 2 m/s" is a factor of 2 in speed, 4 in wavelength (0.64 to 2.55 m as phase speed); [KM10]'s 1 to 4 m/s peak range is a factor of 4 in speed, 16 in wavelength (0.64 to 10 m). Neither source treats this as a transfer function.

"None" reproduces the logged step 1 thresholds exactly [M].

Per-cascade split for the 2.55 m step: light band 0.0055, cascade 0 0.0071, cascades 1 and 2 nothing; moderate band 0.0051, cascade 0 0.0111, cascade 1 0.0005; storm band 0.0055, cascade 0 0.0120, cascade 1 0.0048 [M].

What the numbers show:

- **The band's share grows.** At 2.55 m the band carries 44%, 31% and 25% of the breaking-weighted variance on the light, moderate and storm seas, against 12 to 13% today; at 0.14 m, 20%, 16% and 15%.
- **σ stops being wind-independent.** Today σ is 0.205 to 0.215 on all three seas because the $k^{-3}$ tail carries equal slope variance per octave down to the fixed floor [foam-rework §2.2]. With a fixed cutoff the integration stops at a fixed wavelength while the cut moves with the peak, so σ grows with wind: 0.112, 0.129, 0.149 at 2.55 m. A cutoff at 0.1 $c_p$ moves with the peak too and gives σ of 0.154, 0.149, 0.155, nearly flat again.
- **ε moves towards 1.** With W fixed by Brumer and σ smaller, the threshold marks less compression: at 2.55 m a whitecap is surface compressed by 26 to 29%, against 38 to 53% today. ε stays below 1, so foam still needs compression and never needs folding (λ = 1).
- **The candidates differ by less than the decision might suggest.** From 0.64 to 3.99 m, ε spans 0.635 to 0.739 on the light sea and 0.693 to 0.749 on the storm sea.

## Open questions

- [SM13]'s full text, for its Fig. 3 comparing visible and infrared Λ(c) on the same platform, which [SM15] cites as the basis of the 2 m/s limit; Wiley and the Ifremer mirror refused it.
- [GBG08]'s full text, for where its high-resolution visible Λ(c) drops off in metres per second rather than in $c_p$.
- Whether front speed or phase speed sets the wavelength: [SM15] and [DLM17] take them equal, [MM02] takes $c=0.8c_p$, and [P85 §6] warns that slow fronts are advected by the long waves' orbital velocity, which would make a slow front speed map to an even shorter wave. The 2 m/s candidate is 2.55 m or 3.99 m depending on the convention.
- Whether a cutoff below the 1.28 m floor tile conflicts with the ADR 0011 amendment's wording, which assumes the cutoff is "far above the floor tile".
- No source maps a breaking cutoff onto a Jacobian or slope field; restricting the Dupuy and Bruneton criterion to waves above it is the decision of #40, not a tested method.

## Reproducing the numbers

- Build on main at 6c95bd5 (`pnpm --filter @luovi/tool-scenario sync`, `tsc -b`).
- A throwaway Node script, not committed, loads each reference sea in `packages/tool-validate/src/benchmarks/reference-seas/` with `loadWorld`, takes `waveSpectrum` and `defaultCascadeLayout` from `sim-world`'s dist, sums $\tfrac12(\lambda A_ik_i)^2$ over band components, and integrates $w(k)^2\lambda_c^2k^3F(k)$ in ln k with 4096 trapezoid steps over each annulus. ε is $1+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(W)$ with Brumer's W from `data`'s `whitecapCoverage`, the quantile by Newton on a Simpson cumulative as in `breaking.ts`.

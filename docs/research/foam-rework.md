# Foam rework sources: Dupuy and Bruneton whitecaps, onset and near-to-far coverage

Research for issue #39, 2026-10-24. Question: what the primary sources say a foam layer must do to look like Dupuy and Bruneton's whitecaps at the near camera, start at the right sea state, and agree with the far-field coverage, and what that means for the step 1 foam as built (`render-ocean` `foam.ts` and `surface.ts`, `sim-ocean` `breaking.ts`, `data` `shared/foam.ts` on main at 097d8a3). Facts and options only; the decision belongs to Foam rework design (#40).

## Findings

| #   | Question              | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | The paper's model     | **One field at every distance, no buffer, no decay, an artist threshold.** A point is breaking where the Jacobian determinant of the horizontal map falls below ε [DB12 eq. 3, 4]. Coverage over a pixel footprint is the Gaussian tail of that Jacobian, $W=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\operatorname{erf}((\epsilon-\mu_A)/(\sqrt2\sigma_A))$, with $\mu_A$ and $\sigma_A^2$ read from mipmapped per-pattern textures of $k_i$ and $k_i^2$ in the surface fragment shader [DB12 eq. 6 to 8, §4]. ε is a free parameter that "controls the amount of breaking" [DB12 §3]; the demo exposes it as a "Coverage" slider and its nine saved scenes set it by hand between −0.068 and 0.82 [WC `main.cpp`, `data/scene*.dat`]. Close-range detail is the same formula at mip 0 of the finest 256² pattern (4.3 cm texels on an 11 m tile in the default scene), where $\sigma_A\to0$ and W becomes a sharp step of the interpolated Jacobian; there is no foam texture, noise or buffer [WC `ocean.glsl`]. Foam does not age: decay is listed as future work [DB12 §5]. Nothing in the paper is far-field-only statistics except that, by construction, the same expression averages correctly as the footprint grows (section 1).                                                           |
| 2   | Onset                 | **Field onset is 3.7 to 4.5 m/s; the law is not what makes step 1 foam early.** Detectable whitecapping begins near 3.70 m/s [CAL08], Brumer's combined fit has its offset at 4.23 m/s and its lowest fitted wind at 4.56 m/s [BR17 Table 1], Stramska and Petelski's offsets are 4.47 m/s developed and 6.33 m/s undeveloped [OOWB], and air entrainment is reported from about 3 m/s [DI19, citing Thorpe 1982 and Monahan and O'Muircheartaigh 1986, second-hand]. Brumer gives 0.015% at 4.56 m/s, 0.17% at 6 m/s and 0.49% at 8 m/s, so the far-field target at light winds is small. Two build facts do push foam early: the solve clamps the wind into the law's range, so every wind below 4.56 m/s still targets 0.015% and foam never switches off on a non-flat sea [`breaking.ts` `coverageAt`]; and the near buffer holds about ten times the target (30 to 40% against 3.7% on the storm sea, #26), so the visible near-camera coverage at 8 m/s is plausibly several percent rather than 0.5% (derived, not measured; section 2).                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 3   | Near resolution       | **The paper's sharpness comes from evaluating the Jacobian per pixel at the cascades' own resolution, not from a finer buffer.** In the build, a third to a half of the Jacobian's variance lies in waves shorter than a 0.5 m buffer texel (light sea: 0.021 of 0.042; storm: 0.015 of 0.046, computed from the build's spectrum and cascade layout, section 3), and filtered injection turns that sub-texel variance into a smooth fractional density over whole texels. The far-field path in `surface.ts` already evaluates the paper's W per pixel from the cascade textures; inside the buffer that sharp field is replaced by the 0.5 m one. Sources offer three ways to get near detail: the cascades' Jacobian at render resolution [DB12, WC], a finer buffer, or detail from elsewhere (none of the sources read uses a foam texture or procedural noise). The paper's look also rests on strong choppiness: amplitude factor 2 ("should be one") and per-pattern λ of 0.9 to 2.3 in the default scene with ε = 0.2, so foam marks surface that is close to folding [WC `main.cpp`, `scene1.dat`]. The build at λ = 1 solves ε to 0.47, 0.54 and 0.62 on the light, moderate and storm seas, so its foam marks surface compressed to about half, far from folding. |
| 4   | Near-to-far agreement | **The field definition of W already contains persistence, so a buffer that persists foam on top of an instantaneous threshold solved for total W counts it twice.** Brumer's W is the total coverage of every bright pixel, active and maturing foam and streaks, averaged over 20 minutes [BR17 §3, §5]. Coverage is the sum over whitecaps of each one's area-time integral, split into a growth (active) part and a decay part, $W=W_{growth}+W_{degas}+W_{stab}$ [CA18 eq. 1, 7]. Options the sources support: (a) the paper's own scheme, no persistence, so near and far are one instantaneous field and agree exactly [DB12]; (b) solve ε for the active fraction $W_A$ and let the buffer's decay supply $W_B$, which needs a cited $W_A/W$ [SW16, MO93 second-hand] and an injection that marks only active breaking; (c) keep ε for total W but have the far field scale the instantaneous coverage by the ratio of lifetime to active duration, $1+\tau_{decay}/\tau_{growth}$ [CA18 eq. 7], so both rings carry the same persistence. The build's `max(decayed, coverage)` has no source and no closed-form mean (section 4).                                                                                                                                     |
| 5   | Decay                 | **The Callaghan 2012 full text could not be reached.** Its abstract (read first-hand) gives individual decay times of 0.2 to 10.4 s, an effective (area-weighted mean) decay time of 1.4 to 4.8 s between four observation periods, and small variation in active breaking area between periods [CA12]. Its co-authors' later papers give the periods' winds (11.4, 8.2, 5.7, and 5.7 rising to 13.7 m/s) [CA24 §2.2], define the decay timescale as the area-time integral of the decay phase over the peak area [CA13 eq. 3, CA24 §2.4], and say decay times were longer in the 11.4 m/s period than the 5.7 m/s one [CA13 §4b]. Foam area does not follow a pure exponential; the fitted form is $A(t)=c_0t^n e^{-c_1t}$ [CA24 eq. 3]. No whole-set single value is reachable. Other cited figures: 3.53 s for Monahan et al. 1982's laboratory whitecaps and 3.5 to 4.3 s for stage B (both second-hand), 0.4 to 5 s active and 3 to 30 s residual from infrared imagery (second-hand) (section 5).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 6   | Cost                  | **The paper has no separate foam pass to make cheaper; it evaluates W in the surface fragment shader from textures it already filters.** The build's buffer pass costs 0.81 ms because it sums all 32 band components per texel, 33.6 M `sincos` per frame on a 1024² buffer (#26). The band contributes 0.005 of the Jacobian's 0.042 to 0.046 variance and its shortest wave is 10 to 62 m, 20 to 120 texels, so it varies slowly across a texel (computed, section 3). Options: the surface shader's per-vertex band divergence, which the far field already uses [`surface.ts` `band_divergence`]; evaluating the band on a coarser grid and interpolating (derived here, no source); or dropping the buffer for the paper's per-pixel scheme [DB12 §4].                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

## 1. The paper's model

### 1.1 Where and when a whitecap forms

- **Criterion.** $b(\mathbf p,t)=\Upsilon(\epsilon-j(\mathbf p,t))$, a whitecap wherever b = 1, with j the determinant of $I+\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla\mathbf D_i$ evaluated at the rest position p [DB12 eq. 3, 4]. The criterion "does not reproduce the geometric deformations" of breaking, only the reflectance change [DB12 §3].
- **Threshold and sea state.** ε is a real parameter with no link to wind in the paper. In the demo it is the "Coverage" slider, default 0.2, range −50 to 50 [WC `main.cpp` lines 175, 1458]. The saved scenes pair it with wind and choppiness as follows [WC `data/scene*.dat`, read in the save order of `main.cpp` lines 940 to 966]:

  | Scene   | U10 (m/s) | Ω (inverse wave age) | Amplitude factor | λ per pattern      | ε                  |
  | ------- | --------- | -------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ |
  | 1, 6, 9 | 12        | 2                    | 2                | 2.3, 2.1, 1.3, 0.9 | 0.2, −0.068, 0.182 |
  | 4, 7    | 12        | 2                    | 2                | 2, 1.6, 0.9, 0.7   | 0.138              |
  | 2       | 10        | 2                    | 2.3              | 2.3, 1.9, 1.5, 0.6 | 0.171              |
  | 3       | 20        | 2                    | 2                | 2.3, 1.9, 1.5, 0.6 | 0.421              |
  | 5       | 4         | 0.84                 | 1                | 2.4, 2.4, 2.2, 2   | 0.82               |
  | 8       | 6         | 0.84                 | 1                | 2.1, 1.7, 1, 0.9   | 0.497              |

  The amplitude factor carries the comment "should be one" [WC `main.cpp` line 162]. ε near zero means foam only where the surface is close to folding; the high-wind scenes sit there with doubled amplitudes and λ above 2, and the low-wind scenes raise ε to get any foam at all.

- **Patterns.** Four FFT patterns of 256² [WC `PASSES = 8`]; default tiles 893, 101, 21 and 11 m, other scenes 5409, 503, 51 and 3 to 7 m [WC]. The finest texel is 1.2 to 4.3 cm.

### 1.2 Filtering across scales

- W over a footprint A is the area mean of b [DB12 eq. 5]. Assuming j normal over the footprint (after Ross et al. 2005 for slopes, stated for "large footprints"), W is the erf expression [DB12 eq. 6].
- $\mu_A$ and $\sigma_A^2$ are sums of per-pattern terms: $k_i=\tfrac1n+a_i+b_i+a_ib_i-c_i^2$ and $k_i^2$, each stored in a mipmapped texture, with cross-pattern products dropped as uncorrelated [DB12 eq. 7, 8]. The demo writes `0.25 + Jxx + Jyy + Jxx*Jyy - Jxy*Jxy` and its square per pattern and generates mips [WC `whitecap_precompute.glsl`; `main.cpp` lines 1128 to 1129], and the surface shader takes $\sigma^2=\sum\overline{k_i^2}-\sum\overline{k_i}^2$ at hardware-selected mip and anisotropy (16x on the foam textures) [WC `ocean.glsl` lines 315 to 325; `main.cpp` line 1634].
- **Near and far.** There is one expression for every distance. Near the camera, the hardware picks mip 0, $\overline{k_i^2}\approx\overline{k_i}^2$, $\sigma_A\to0$, and W becomes the step of the bilinearly interpolated Jacobian: sharp-edged foam at the finest pattern's texel scale. Far away the footprint covers many texels and W is the expected coverage. The far-field "statistics" are the near-field detail averaged, not a separate model.

### 1.3 Texture, detail and shading at close range

- Radiance $R\simeq WR_F+R_C+R_W$, foam Lambertian at reflectance 0.4 [DB12 eq. 2, §2]; the demo adds `W * l * 0.4` with l the sun and sky irradiance over π, on top of the water rather than replacing a fraction of it [WC `ocean.glsl` lines 327 to 330].
- No foam texture, bubble pattern, noise or second-scale detail is used. The paper lists "a richer shading model" as future work [DB12 §5].
- A screen-space foam program in `foam.glsl` (with a commented-out pointwise erf test) is not loaded by the demo [WC].

### 1.4 Ageing

None. Whitecap decay "can last up to several seconds", citing Koepke 1984, and is future work, as is masking at grazing angles [DB12 §5].

Caveats the sources state: [BR17]'s W is total coverage, active plus maturing plus streaks, averaged over 20 minutes and binned [BR17 §3, §5]; its waters were cold (5 to 8 °C, never above 14 °C), which the authors link to a weaker wind dependence [BR17 §5]; hourly W scatters by up to two orders of magnitude at a given wind [BR17 §5]; [CAL08]'s video W also includes stage A and stage B [CAL08].

### 2.2 The step 1 solve against it

- At sea-state build, `breakingThreshold` takes Brumer at the generating wind, clamped to 4.56 to 25.1 m/s, and returns $\epsilon=1+\sigma_J\,\Phi^{-1}(W)$ with $\sigma_J$ the linearised Jacobian's standard deviation over band plus default cascades [`sim-ocean/src/breaking.ts`].
- Logged values on the reference seas [M]:

  | Sea      | U10 | Target W | $\sigma_J$ | $\Phi^{-1}(W)$ | ε     |
  | -------- | --- | -------- | ---------- | -------------- | ----- |
  | light    | 8   | 0.49%    | 0.205      | −2.59          | 0.470 |
  | moderate | 12  | 1.36%    | 0.207      | −2.21          | 0.542 |
  | storm    | 20  | 3.71%    | 0.215      | −1.79          | 0.616 |

  $\sigma_J$ is almost the same on all three seas: on the spectrum's $k^{-3}$ tail every factor of k carries equal slope variance, and the cascade chain always reaches the fixed 1.28 m floor tile, so the short waves set $\sigma_J$ whatever the wind (derived from the comment in `breaking.ts` and the split in section 3).

- **What the law contributes at onset.** 0.015% at 4.56 m/s and 0.05% at 5 m/s: little. Below 4.56 m/s the clamp holds the target at 0.015% rather than zero, so any non-flat sea foams somewhere [`breaking.ts` `coverageAt`]. Whether that is visible depends on the near buffer.
- **What the build adds.** The near buffer's steady coverage measured 30 to 40% against the 3.7% storm target [#26]. If the same factor holds at 8 m/s, near-camera coverage is around 5% against 0.49% (derived, not measured). Brumer's offset puts essentially no foam below about 4.5 m/s and well under 1% at Beaufort 4; a buffer that multiplies the target by ten makes 8 m/s look like a gale.
- Brumer is fitted against the 10 m neutral wind; the solve takes the generating wind, which ignores stability, as `data` already records.

## 3. Near resolution

### 3.1 Where the Jacobian's variance lives in the build

The divergence variance per cascade, $\lambda^2\int k^2F\,dk$ over each annulus, on the default layout [M]:

| Sea      | Band   | Cascade 0              | Cascade 1              | Cascade 2                | Total | Shorter than 0.5 m |
| -------- | ------ | ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------ | ----- | ------------------ |
| light    | 0.0055 | 0.0100 (10.3 to 1.5 m) | 0.0103 (1.5 to 0.21 m) | 0.0163 (0.21 to 0.01 m)  | 0.042 | about 0.021        |
| moderate | 0.0051 | 0.0111 (28 to 2.8 m)   | 0.0115 (2.8 to 0.28 m) | 0.0153 (0.28 to 0.013 m) | 0.043 | about 0.017        |
| storm    | 0.0055 | 0.0120 (62 to 6.2 m)   | 0.0123 (6.2 to 0.62 m) | 0.0164 (0.62 to 0.029 m) | 0.046 | about 0.015        |

The last column splits a cascade by ln k, which is exact on the flat $k^3F$ tail. A third to a half of the variance that decides where foam sits comes from waves shorter than one buffer texel, and the last cascade reaches centimetre wavelengths.

### 3.2 What that does to the near buffer

- Filtered injection evaluates, per 0.5 m texel, the band exactly and each cascade's divergence mean and variance from the mip matching the texel [`foam.ts`]. The sub-texel variance enters as $\sigma^2$ in the Gaussian tail, so a texel gets a fraction, not a 0 or 1. That fraction is non-zero over wide areas wherever the resolved mean comes within a few $\sigma$ of ε.
- The surface reads the buffer bilinearly at the undisplaced position and uses density as coverage [`surface.ts`]. The result cannot carry any shape smaller than about two texels (1 m).

### 3.3 What the sources offer for near detail

- **The cascades' own Jacobian at render resolution** [DB12, WC]. This is how the paper gets its close-range look: W per pixel at mip 0 of the finest pattern. The build's far path already does this from the same textures (`surface.ts`, `coverage_below(threshold, jacobian, jacobian_variance)`), so near the camera the sharp field exists and is masked by the buffer.
- **A finer buffer.** No source sizes one. A texel matching cascade 2's 1.5 cm on 1024² would cover 15 m.
- **Detail from elsewhere.** None of [DB12], [WC], or the production talks in the ocean survey (Atlas, Sea of Thieves) uses a foam texture or procedural noise for whitecaps; Atlas and Sea of Thieves blur their buffer, which removes detail rather than adding it [ocean survey §3.2]. Any texture or noise would have no primary source in this set.
- **Which waves should whitecap.** Whitecaps are a surface expression of breaking with air entrainment [DI19; CA18 §1]; Phillips 1985's breaking-crest distribution Λ(c) ties active whitecaps to breaking crests above a minimum speed (second-hand via [AH] §2), and Kleiss and Melville 2010 find the breaking dissipation moments peaking at crest speeds of about 5 to 7 m/s (abstract, second-hand). A 1 to 3 cm wave of cascade 2 has a phase speed near 0.2 m/s and does not entrain air. On a Gaussian spectrum the Jacobian's lower tail is dominated by these short waves (table above), so the build's foam sits where centimetre waves happen to compress, not on the crests of breaking waves. This is an inference from the sources, not a statement any of them makes about the Jacobian criterion.
- **Choppiness.** The paper's foam marks near-folding surface (ε about 0 to 0.2 at λ up to 2.3 and doubled amplitude); the build's marks surface at about half its rest area (ε 0.47 to 0.62 at λ = 1) [section 1.1, 2.2].

## 4. Near-to-far agreement

### 4.1 What W measures

- Brumer's W is the fraction of thresholded bright pixels, so it includes maturing foam and streaks, and is a 20 minute mean [BR17 §3, §5]. [CAL08]'s W likewise contains stage A and stage B [CAL08].
- Coverage is the sum of each whitecap's area-time integral over the observed area and time, $W=\sum_i\int_0^\infty A_i\,dt/(A_{obs}T_{obs})$, which splits into a growth part (to the time of peak area) and a decay part, the decay further into plume degassing and surfactant stabilisation: $W=W_{growth}+W_{degas}+W_{stab}$, with $\tau_{growth}$ and $\tau_{degas}$ each the integral of its phase over the peak area [CA18 eq. 1 to 8]. [CA18] approximates $W_{growth}\approx W_A$ and uses [SW16]'s field ratio $W_A/W$, which falls with wind speed [CA18 Fig. 5].
- Stage A whitecaps are "surface manifestations of plunging aerated plumes" (Monahan and Woolf, quoted in [CA18 §1]); $W_A$ is the foam of actively breaking waves [CA18 §1; SW16]. The stage B share has been reported as 1.5 to 40 times stage A (second-hand, attributed to Callaghan 2013 in a search summary, not confirmed).

### 4.2 What the build does

Each frame, per texel: decay by $e^{-\Delta t/\tau}$, then `max` with the instantaneous filtered coverage, then `max` with stamps [`foam.ts`]. The far field shows the instantaneous filtered coverage at the lifetime reflectance [`surface.ts`]. The threshold was solved so the far field's instantaneous spatial mean equals Brumer's total W. Then:

- The far field already shows total W with no persistence, so it agrees with Brumer.
- The near field adds persistence on top of an injection already calibrated to total W, which double counts the decay part (a reading of [CA18 eq. 7] against the code; #26 calls it "Max-persistence double counts").
- A `max` of a fluctuating fraction with memory τ tends towards the fraction's running maximum over about τ, not its mean times a lifetime factor. Most of the fluctuation comes from cascade 2's short, fast-cycling waves (periods under a second), so in 3 s a texel samples many independent exceedances (derived from section 3.1; no source models this operator).

### 4.3 Options with sources

- **(a) No persistence** [DB12]. Near and far are one instantaneous field and match by construction; trails behind breaking crests are lost. The paper itself names the missing decay as its first limitation.
- **(b) Active threshold plus decay.** Solve ε for $W_A$, inject only active breaking, let decay produce $W_B$, so the buffer's steady mean is $W_A(1+\tau_{decay}/\tau_{growth})$ in the sense of [CA18 eq. 7]. Needs a cited $W_A/W$ or $W_A(U)$ ([SW16] fits it, numbers not read; Monahan 1993's regression reaches us only garbled and second-hand), a cited $\tau_{growth}$ or active duration ([CA24] reports $t_{A_o}$ and $\tau_{growth}$ distributions in figures only; 0.4 to 5 s active from infrared, second-hand), and an additive or one-shot injection whose steady mean is analytic. The far field then shows $W_A$ times the same factor, applied to the instantaneous tail.
- **(c) Total threshold, persistence in both.** Keep ε for total W, and solve the injection so that the buffer's steady mean equals the far field's W, for instance injecting at a rate whose product with the lifetime is W (derived from [CA18 eq. 1]). This needs no $W_A/W$ but loses the active and residual split.
- **(d) Reflectance split.** Whatever the coverage split, [DI19] gives about 0.50 for stage A and 0.18 for thin residual foam, and Koepke 1984's 22% lifetime average (as reported by [DI19]) already folds the stage B dimming into one number for coverage that is not tracked. Option (b) would pair naturally with 0.50 at injection falling towards 0.18; (a) and (c) with 0.22.

## 5. Decay

### 5.1 Callaghan 2012, what is and is not reachable

- **First-hand, abstract** [CA12]: 552 whitecaps, Martha's Vineyard Coastal Observatory, 10 days in 2008, four observation periods; individual decay times 0.2 to 10.4 s; decay time grows with peak foam patch area within each period; for a given size it varies by a factor of 2 to 5; the effective decay time, "the area-weighted mean decay time", varies between periods from 1.4 to 4.8 s; active breaking area varied little between periods; surfactants and plume differences are the suggested causes.
- **First-hand, co-authors' later papers.** Period winds 11.4, 8.2, 5.7, and 5.7 rising to 13.7 m/s, reproduced from [CA12] Table 1 [CA24 §2.2]. Decay timescale defined as $A_o^{-1}\int_{t_{A_o}}^{t_2}A\,dt$, the integral method, which with an exponential tail equals the e-folding time [CA13 eq. 3; CA24 §2.4]. Longer decay times in the 11.4 m/s period than the 5.7 m/s one [CA13 §4b]. Foam area fits $A(t)=c_0t^ne^{-c_1t}$ rather than a pure exponential, and surfactant stabilisation lengthens the tail beyond about $2t_{A_o}$ [CA24 eq. 3, §2.3]; field stabilisation factors were 1.50, 1.66, 1.09 and 1.49 for the four periods [CA24 §3].
- **Not reachable.** Which period had 1.4 s and which 4.8 s (the above suggests 1.4 s at 5.7 m/s and 4.8 s at 11.4 m/s, an inference), any whole-set mean or median, and the per-period table.

### 5.3 Against the build

`data` holds τ = 3 s as an estimate inside [CA12]'s 1.4 to 4.8 s, the abstract's range [`data/src/shared/foam.ts`]. The full text would not change the range. What the sources add: the field quantity is a foam area timescale, not a density e-folding; it grows with whitecap size and probably with wind; and the curve is growth then decay, not an instant jump followed by exponential decay.

## 6. Cost

- [DB12] has two passes: FFT plus a per-pattern precompute of $k_i$ and $k_i^2$ at the pattern's 256² with mips, then the surface draw that evaluates W in the fragment shader [DB12 §4; WC]. Whole frames ran under 10 ms at 1280 x 720 on a GTX 560 Ti with eight RGBA16F textures, 5.33 MB with mips [DB12 §4, Fig. 2]. There is no per-texel band sum because the paper has no analytic band.
- The build's buffer pass sums 32 band components per texel on 1024², 33.6 M `sincos` per frame, measured at 0.81 ms on an M1 Pro [#26].
- The band contributes 0.005 of about 0.044 Jacobian variance, and its shortest wavelength is the cut (10.3, 28 and 62 m), 20 to 120 texels, so its divergence changes little across one texel [M].
- Options, none of which a source prescribes for this split: take the band's divergence from the surface vertex shader, which already computes it for the far field [`surface.ts` `band_divergence`]; evaluate the band on a grid a few times coarser and interpolate (derived here); or move injection into the surface pass as the paper does, which removes the buffer pass entirely but also the persistence that needs it.

## Open questions

- [CA12]'s full text for the per-period effective decay times and any whole-set value; a library copy would settle it.
- [SW16]'s $W_A(U)$ and $W_A/W$ fits, and [MO93]'s stage A regression, if option (b) is pursued; both are paywalled.
- Whether a Jacobian criterion can be restricted to waves that whitecap (for instance excluding the last cascade from injection, or weighting by phase speed after Phillips 1985). No source tests this; it would need a scenario with a numeric target.
- [BR17]'s cold-water caveat against Baltic summer temperatures; the sources here give no correction.
- Stamps at undisplaced positions need the inverse horizontal displacement; no source here gives a method.

## Reproducing the numbers

- Thresholds and target coverage: `node packages/tool-validate/dist/cli.js --filter ocean.band` on main at 097d8a3, then the `breakingThreshold`, `targetCoverage` and `cutWavelength` logs in `packages/tool-validate/report.json`.
- Variance split: a throwaway Node script, not committed, that loads each reference sea with `loadWorld`, sums $\tfrac12(\lambda A_ik_i)^2$ over band components, and integrates $\lambda_c^2k^3F(k)$ in ln k with 512 trapezoid steps over each annulus of `defaultCascadeLayout`, as `breaking.ts` does with 256. Its total reproduces the logged ε to three decimals.
- Brumer values: $0.0738(U-4.23)^{1.42}$ at the stated winds.

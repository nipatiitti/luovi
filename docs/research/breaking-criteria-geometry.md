# Breaking criteria and the geometry of their excursion sets

Research for the foam rework follow-up, 2026-10-24. Question: why thresholding the linearised breaking Jacobian of the build's sea (JONSWAP-TMA, Donelan-Banner spreading, band plus FFT cascades, ADR 0005 and the ADR 0011 amendment) gives round soft blobs where real whitecaps are thin streaks along crests; what excursion-set theory predicts for the shape of the regions above a threshold of any linear field of that sea; which breaking criteria computable per point from a linear sea select crests narrowly and match measured coverage across winds; and which combination to prototype. Facts and options only; the decision belongs to a later ticket. Builds on `research/foam-rework` (#39) and `research/breaking-cutoff` (#42).

Notation: σ² the variance of the thresholded field f, Λ = E[∇f ∇fᵀ] its gradient covariance, x the mean wave direction, y along the crests, ν = Φ⁻¹(1 − W) the threshold in standard deviations for coverage W (Brumer's 0.486%, 1.357%, 3.707% on the light, moderate and storm seas give ν = 2.59, 2.21, 1.79). A cutoff L_c keeps only waves at least L_c long (step weight), as in the breaking cutoff research.

| 1   | Geometry of excursion sets | **A thresholded stationary Gaussian field at 0.5 to 4% coverage makes blobs about a fifth of its own characteristic wavelength across, with the aspect ratio of its gradient covariance; thin crest-long streaks need an angular spread three or more times narrower than any wind sea has, so for a single linear field of this sea round patches are inevitable.** Mean blob area is W/χ with χ the Euler characteristic density, which gives a blob diameter of about 0.45 L_x/ν, L_x = 2πσ/√Λ_xx; at high levels each excursion is close to an ellipse with axes in the ratio √(λ_max/λ_min) of Λ, its long axis along the direction of least gradient variance (section 1). | 2   | Criteria per point         | **Every pointwise criterion that is linear in the surface is a reweighting of the spectrum and inherits the result above; the ones that select crests narrowly are nonlinear, and no cited threshold reproduces Brumer's wind dependence by itself.** The linearised Jacobian is, in deep water, exactly the vertical acceleration criterion of Snyder and Kennedy (J − 1 = λ a_z/g); with no cutoff the solved thresholds are 0.53, 0.46 and 0.38 g, and a fixed 0.5 g gives 0.74 to 1.0% on all three seas against Brumer's 0.49 to 3.7%. With the 2.55 m cutoff the solved threshold is nearly constant, 0.29, 0.29 and 0.27 g. Geometric slope thresholds (30° inviscid, 60° with surface tension) and the kinematic u/c threshold (0.85 nonlinear, 0.32 to 0.38 linear-equivalent) are reached by a linear sea with probability 10⁻⁴ down to 10⁻¹⁷. | 3   | What to prototype          | **Multi-scale crest strips: per octave band from the breaking cutoff to the peak, an envelope gate (the linear form of the kinematic and group criteria) times a crest-phase strip, with one width solved so the mean coverage equals Brumer.** It is the only construction tried that gives thin crest-aligned streaks (aspect 4.7 to 16, alignment with the crests 0.93 to 0.96 on a scale where 1 is crest-parallel and 0.64 random) at exact Brumer coverage on all three seas. Cited: the cutoff, the coverage law, envelope gating by group steepness, the linear crest slowdown. Picture-only: the octave split, the strip's offset towards the front face, edge texture, and (until Banner et al. 2002's saturation thresholds are read) the per-band gate fraction. A cheaper fallback that keeps all of ADR 0011's linear machinery is to threshold J_xx instead of the trace: crest-aligned, aspect about 3.5, still blobs (section 4).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

## 1. Excursion-set theory

### 1.1 Densities per unit area

For a stationary zero-mean Gaussian field f with variance σ² and gradient covariance Λ, thresholded at u = νσ (the region f > u; for J the region below ε is the same with f = 1 − J):

| Quantity                                                                     | Expression                                                                                            | Origin                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Area fraction                                                                | Ψ(ν) = 1 − Φ(ν) = W                                                                                   | Definition                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Level crossings per length along direction e                                 | (1/π) √(eᵀΛe)/σ · e^(−ν²/2) (both directions)                                                         | Rice; Longuet-Higgins 1957 eq. 2.2.5, as restated in [ST21 eq. 3]                                                                                                                 |
| Boundary length per area                                                     | P = e^(−ν²/2)/(2σ) · (1/π)∫₀^π √(e_φᵀΛe_φ) dφ, isotropic: √λ₂ e^(−ν²/2)/(2σ)                          | The crossing rate averaged over direction by the Cauchy-Crofton formula (derived here; agrees with the Gaussian kinematic formula's L₁, second-hand)                              |
| Euler characteristic per area (number of blobs minus holes; blobs at high ν) | χ = (2π)^(−3/2) √det Λ /σ² · ν e^(−ν²/2)                                                              | Gaussian kinematic formula, Adler and Taylor (second-hand)                                                                                                                        |
| Mean blob area                                                               | A = W/χ ≈ L_x L_y/(2π ν²) for large ν, with L_i = 2πσ/√Λ_ii                                           | From the two above and Mills' ratio Ψ(ν) ≈ φ(ν)/ν                                                                                                                                 |
| Blob shape at high ν                                                         | Ellipse with semi-axes ∝ Λ^(−1/2): aspect √(λ_max/λ_min), long axis along the least-varying direction | Slepian model: given f = u at a maximum, the mean Hessian is −(u/σ²)Λ (Adler 1981, second-hand); [CH21] derives the contour Minkowski tensor's expectation for anisotropic fields |

Two consequences carry the rest of this note:

- **Size is a fixed fraction of the field's own wavelength.** With L_x the field's characteristic along-wave wavelength, an isotropic blob's equal-area diameter is about 0.45 L_x/ν, a fifth of L_x at the three seas' ν. Nothing in the threshold changes this: a lower coverage gives fewer, slightly smaller blobs, not thinner ones.
- **Shape is the field's short-crestedness.** The aspect √(Λ_xx/Λ_yy) is the ratio of the field's mean crest length to its mean wavelength. For a spectrum S_f(k, θ) it is √(⟨cos²θ⟩/⟨sin²θ⟩) under the weight k² S_f. An aspect of 5 needs an rms angular spread near 11°, an aspect of 10 near 6°.

### 1.2 Which fields

A pointwise criterion that is linear in the surface thresholds a field whose spectrum is w(k, θ) F(k, θ) for some weight w: div D has w = λ²k²; the deep-water vertical acceleration a_z/g has w = (ω²/g)² = k² tanh²(kd), identical to div D at λ = 1 (to three digits on the reference seas); the along-wave Jacobian component ∂D_x/∂x has w = k² cos⁴θ; the along-wave slope ∂η/∂x, k² cos²θ; the Laplacian of η, k⁴. Weights in k move the characteristic wavelength; weights in θ narrow the spread, at most to the spread of F itself times the weight.

### 2.2 Monte Carlo check

The masks also show what the renderer then does. With a 2.55 to 3.99 m cutoff the trace's blobs cluster along the dominant crests, because the band carries 25 to 54% of the breaking-weighted variance and decides where the short waves' excursions can occur; a cluster on the storm sea is 10 to 30 m across by eye and made of metre-scale speckle. The per-pixel Gaussian tail over a footprint of a metre or more turns each cluster into one soft patch, which is the round blob in `storm.3.99.png`. Without a cutoff the speckle is centimetric and the filtered field is a fine grey froth, as in `storm.0.14.png`.

### 2.3 Can any thresholded linear field give crest-long thin streaks at about 1%?

No, not from this sea. Width and length are each about a fifth of the field's own wavelength in that direction, so a streak with aspect 5 to 15 (our reading of the reference photographs, not a cited figure) needs Λ_xx/Λ_yy of 25 to 225, an rms spread of 4° to 11° in the field's weighted spectrum. The dominant band's is about 20° and every shorter band's is wider. Narrowing by an extra cos^(2n)θ weight is a choice of field, not physics, and a weight strong enough for aspect 5 would discard most of the short-crested sea. A thresholded Gaussian field can be crest-aligned (J_xx, front slope) but its blobs stay at aspect 3 to 4 in the Monte Carlo measure, about 2.2 to 2.7 at high level.

Thin streaks need a non-Gaussian construction: an intersection of a crest locator (narrow along x) with a gate that is long along y, or a ratio such as u/c. Both are nonlinear in the surface, so the footprint mean is no longer exact from one mipmapped mean and variance.

Notes on the rows:

- **Acceleration.** The identity J − 1 = λ a_z/g (deep water, linear) means the build already implements Snyder and Kennedy's breaking variable, with λ = 1. [SSK83] concludes the threshold is "approximately 0.5g", from 2292 photographed whitecaps at winds up to 10 m/s, but measured accelerations only below 2f_p; on that band our seas would need 0.15 to 0.21 g. [LH85]'s bound on the real acceleration, −0.39 g, is the steepest symmetric wave's and says nothing of an unsteady crest. With the 2.55 m cutoff a single threshold near 0.28 g reproduces Brumer within about 30% on all three seas (0.62%, 1.5%, 3.0% against 0.49%, 1.36%, 3.7%), while without a cutoff the solved threshold drifts from 0.53 to 0.38 g: the cutoff is what makes one physical threshold fit the law. This is our observation, not a source's claim.
- **Geometric criteria.** [BA18 §4.1] and [CA26] find local steepness does not separate breaking from recurrent crests; [BO25] gives the interface angle a robust 60° threshold with surface tension. All of them are defined on the nonlinear crest moments before breaking. A linear Gaussian sea's slopes reach 30° at 10⁻⁴ of the surface or less.
- **Kinematic and energy-flux criteria.** B_x = u_x/c_x ≥ 0.85 to 0.86 at the crest, with c the crest point speed, holds across 2D and 3D, deep and intermediate depth [BA18 §5.3], shallow water [DE20], and with surface tension [BO25]. Crest speed at a focusing group maximum is about 0.8 of the linear phase speed, so the threshold is u/c₀ ≈ 0.68 [BA18 §5.3]. The slowdown has a linear origin: c ≈ c₀ − β a_xx/(a k²), lowest at the envelope maximum [BA14 eq. 2, 3], so a linear field can supply the crest speed. [ST21] derives the joint density of crest speed and orbital velocity at the local maxima of a linear field from Longuet-Higgins 1957 and needs a linear-equivalent threshold A_lin = u_eq/c_eq = 0.382; it underpredicts measured dominant breaking with that value and fits 0.24 to one dataset [ST21 §4]. Evaluated per point, u/c of a linear band is its envelope steepness a k, whose Rayleigh scale σ_b k_b is 0.043 to 0.064 per octave on our seas [M], so any cited value is out of reach and the threshold becomes a calibrated number like ε today.
- **Group criteria.** [BBY00]: breaking probability of dominant waves "strongly correlated" with significant steepness, a threshold below which negligible breaking occurs, then near-quadratic growth; the same dependence holds in a higher-frequency band. [BGF02]: in bands from 1 to 2.48 f_p, a common threshold of directionally normalised saturation. On the reference seas ε is almost the same (the seas are fetch-developed JONSWAP), so b_T is 3.4 to 4.0% at 8, 12 and 20 m/s while Brumer's W grows 7.6-fold. The storm sea's W/b_T of 1.0 means the dominant waves alone cannot carry Brumer's coverage. [ST15, in SC16 §3] finds W best predicted by the mean square slope of the equilibrium range (√2 to 5 f_m) normalised by directional spread and bandwidth (R² = 0.75), not by the dominant waves.

## 4. What to prototype

Ranked by how well they meet "thin, crest-aligned, Brumer at all three seas, cited where possible":

1. **Multi-scale crest strips.** For each octave band b from the breaking cutoff to the peak: the analytic signal P_b = Σ a e^(i(k·x − ωt + φ)) over the band's components folded onto the half plane of the mean wave direction, its envelope A_b = |P_b| and crest phase φ_b = arg P_b. A point is whitecap where some band is gated, A_b k_b above the band's gate threshold, and near that band's crest, cos φ_b above c. Coverage W = 1 − Π_b(1 − g_b arccos(c)/π) for independent bands with gate fractions g_b, so one number c is solved from Brumer in closed form at sea-state build, as ε is today; the gate thresholds follow from the Rayleigh law of a Gaussian band's envelope (the band's fixed-amplitude components need the random-phasor sum's distribution, also deterministic). What is cited: the breaking cutoff [breaking-cutoff research]; Brumer's W; gating by group steepness [BBY00, BGF02] and its kinematic reading u/c = ak with the linear crest slowdown [BA14, BA18]; the crest as the breaking site [BA18, SK83]. What is picture-only until sources settle it: the octave split; g_b (BBY's b_T of 3.4 to 4.0% for every band is a defensible start, since [BBY00] reports the same dependence in a higher band, but the per-band threshold of [BGF02] needs its full text); the strip's offset onto the front face and its edge texture. Cost: the band part is free (the vertex stage already evaluates sin and cos of every component, so P_b is a second accumulator); each tail octave needs a complex inverse FFT channel, two to five at 256² for a 2.55 m cutoff (the octaves between the cut at 3 k_p and the cutoff). Far field: the long bands' gates are resolved and evaluated exactly per pixel; the short bands enter by their expected coverage g_b arccos(c)/π, which replaces the Gaussian tail of a sum. Nothing here enters authoritative state.
2. **J_xx instead of the trace.** One channel change: ∂D_x/∂x along the scenario's mean wave direction, with the cutoff. It keeps everything ADR 0011 relies on (linear, mip-exact mean and variance, Gaussian solve, the quick-tier variance benchmark) and makes blobs crest-aligned with aspect about 3.5. It does not make them thin, and its physics is only that onset criteria are formulated along the propagation direction [BA18 §5.2]. A sensible first step that the strips would supersede.
3. **Keep the trace, read it as acceleration.** Rename nothing in the code, but record that the threshold is a downward acceleration of 0.27 to 0.29 g with the 2.55 m cutoff, close to constant across the three seas. Useful for the ADR's justification; it changes no pixel.

Not worth prototyping: the determinant and the smallest eigenvalue (nonlinear, no shape gain over J_xx); the slope magnitude (two flanks, not the crest); curvature (dominated by the shortest kept waves, isotropic); any criterion that thresholds a linear field at a cited physical value (section 3.1: either flat across winds or unreachable).

## Open questions

- [BGF02]'s full text, for the directionally normalised saturation threshold per band, which would turn the strips' per-band gate from a choice into a citation; [BBY00]'s full text to confirm the fit's coefficients first-hand.
- [SK83] Parts I to III full texts: Part I defines "three classes of geometrical moments" of whitecap events from the directional spectrum, the closest prior work to section 1, and Part III compares them with 2292 photographed whitecaps. AMS refused automated access; a browser download would settle it.
- A cited aspect ratio or width distribution for active whitecaps. [SC16 §4] uses aspect ratio to identify active breaking but gives no value; the 5 to 15 here is our reading of the reference photographs.
- Whether the strips' phase width should grow with wind (2°, 4°, 10° here at a fixed gate) or the gate should: Brumer fixes only the product. The active fraction W_A/W of Scanlon and Ward 2016 (paywalled, see the foam rework research) would split it.
- The mean wave direction for J_xx and for the half-plane fold is global per sea; a swell crossing the wind sea, or a sea with spreading near 90° in the shortest bands, would need per-band directions.

## Reproducing the numbers

Throwaway scripts in a scratch directory, not committed, on this branch's parent (main at 6c95bd5):

- **Spectra.** After `pnpm install`, `pnpm --filter @luovi/tool-scenario sync` and `tsc -b`, a Node script loads each reference sea in `packages/tool-validate/src/benchmarks/reference-seas/` with `sim-world`'s `loadWorld`, evaluates `waveSpectrum(sea).directional` on a 1200 (log k, from k_p/8 to the last cascade's Nyquist) by 721 (θ) grid, and writes it with the band components (k_x, k_y, amplitude, phase, ω) and `defaultCascadeLayout`.
- **Theory (section 2.1, 3.1).** Python with NumPy and SciPy integrates w(k, θ) F k² over ln k and θ above the cut, adds ½A² w for each band component, and applies section 1.1's formulas. BBY's ε uses the variance of the 0.7 to 1.3 ω_p band with k_p from the build; octave-band steepness is √(m₀,b) √(k_lo k_hi).
- **Monte Carlo (section 2.2, 3.2).** One seeded realisation per sea on a 2048² periodic lattice, tail amplitudes (ξ_r + iξ_i)√((F(k) + F(−k)) Δk²) on the half plane k_x > 0 so that every derived field is the real part of one complex sum, band components added exactly at their phases; every field is a spectral multiplier on the same realisation; thresholds are the empirical quantiles at Brumer's W; blobs by `skimage.measure.label` (8-connected) and `regionprops`. Multi-scale strips: octave bands [k_p 2^(j − ½), k_p 2^(j + ½)] up to 2π/2.55 m, one global quantile of A_b k_b over all bands as the gate, c solved on the realised field.

# How asymmetric are real wind waves, and which waves carry it

Research note, 2026-11-15. Follow-up to `docs/research/baltic-wave-shape.md` (branch `research/baltic-wave-shape`), sections 4 and 5, which found only a wind-tank fit for front-back asymmetry and ruled forward lean out of v1. This note looks for field numbers, says which waves carry the lean in a young fetch-limited sea, and lists the models. Facts and options only.

Owner's seas, for reference: wave age c_p/U10 0.58 to 0.78 (U10/c_p 1.28 to 1.72), U10 6 to 12 m/s, Hs 0.3 to 2 m, Tp 3 to 6 s, depth 20 to 60 m. With a drag coefficient of 1.2 to 1.27 × 10⁻³ (u* ≈ 0.035 U10, an assumption, not from a source read here) the peak has u*/c_p = 0.044 to 0.061 [M].

Tags: [M] = computed for this note (scripts described at the end). "Read in full", "sections", "abstract" say how much of each source was read. Second-hand numbers are marked as such.

## 1. Field measurements of front-back asymmetry

### Definitions in use

- **Statistical asymmetry** As = ⟨H(η)³⟩/η_rms³, H the Hilbert transform; negative means forward-leaning [AG05 eq. 2, LE95]. It equals the imaginary part of the bispectrum integrated, as skewness is the real part [AG05 §1].
- **Geometric, per wave**: Babanin's As = b1/b2 − 1 from horizontal distances on the profile [BA10 eq. 2.16]; Stansell's Ac = (T3 − T4)/(T3 + T4) from the crest's front and back quarter periods, and At for troughs [ST03 eq. 7, 8]; Kjeldsen and Myrhaug's λ = L''/L' (they call it the "vertical asymmetry factor", asymmetry about a vertical axis) and ε = η'/L' [KM80 eq. 1]. Stansell notes λ = T4/T3 and that, unlike λ, Ac is symmetric about zero for a linear Gaussian sea.
- A linear Gaussian sea is "stochastically symmetric, both in the vertical and in the horizontal direction" [LB09 abstract]: individual waves in it do lean, either way, but not on average. Random second-order theory also has zero average front-back asymmetry [ST03 §1].

### North Sea storms [ST03]

Ten severe storms at North Alwyn (130 m), three Thorn EMI laser altimeters at 5 Hz on the platform, upwind sensor only, 1 879 twenty-minute records with Hm0 > 3 m, 217 083 zero-down-crossing waves, the largest 24.2 m; "essentially uni-modal wind-driven seas without significant swell" (§3, Table 1). Records were grouped by H̄/Tz² in three bins (0.044 to 0.064 m s⁻²). Results (Fig. 5, local regressions with 95% intervals; values read off the figure):

| H* = H/H̄    | 0.5    | 1     | 1.5   | 2      | 2.5   | 3     | 3.5   |
| ----------- | ------ | ----- | ----- | ------ | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Ac (crest)  | +0.005 | −0.01 | −0.03 | −0.045 | −0.06 | −0.07 | −0.08 |
| At (trough) | −0.005 | 0     | +0.02 | +0.035 | +0.05 | +0.06 | +0.07 |

"The average horizontal asymmetry is not discernible among small waves, but among waves of mean wave height and greater, it seems to increase with wave height" (§8). For large waves the quarter periods order as τ3 < τ2 < τ4 < τ1, i.e. the crest's front face is the shortest quarter and the leading half of the trough ahead of it (the gentle back of the previous crest) the longest (§5); small waves (H* < 1) are horizontally symmetric and vertically asymmetric "like a Stokes wave". The dependence is the same in all three steepness bins. Wave age and wind are not given. What it means geometrically [M]: at Ac = −0.05 the crest's front face is 10% steeper than its back; at −0.08, 17%.

Second-hand via [ST03 §1]: Vinje and Haver (Gullfaks lasers) and Jonathan et al. (Tern) found average horizontal asymmetry "more or less zero". Their own numbers were not read.

### Lake George and the Black Sea [AG05, YB06, BA10]

[AG05] (read in full): wire-probe arrays; Lake George strongly forced (U10/c_p > 5, kph = 1.3, fp > 0.5 Hz); Black Sea well developed (U10/c_p ≈ 1, fp ≈ 0.2 Hz). Breaking rates under 10% (lake) and 5% (Black Sea) of dominant waves. Skewness and asymmetry vary strongly between probes 30 cm apart and over time; Fig. 1 shows record means of As between about −0.08 and 0 and of skewness between 0 and 0.55 for Lake George; Fig. 2 a 400-period running asymmetry between about −0.15 and 0 over two hours. "We found both positive and negative asymmetries. These are correlated with increased and decreased wind energy input." No Black Sea asymmetry values are given.

[YB06] (§2 read): one stationary 20-min Lake George record, U10 = 19.8 m/s, fp = 0.36 Hz, Hs = 0.45 m, depth 1.1 m, about half the dominant waves breaking, segmented by hydrophone into breaking and non-breaking stretches. Breaking: skewness 0.45, kurtosis 3.34, asymmetry −0.186. Non-breaking: 0.31, 2.96, −0.017 [eq. 11]. [M]: kh = 0.84, L = 8.2 m, U10/c_p = 6.7, u*/c_p ≈ 0.28. This is depth-limited, shallower than the owner's water; the skewness is raised by the depth.

[BA10] (§§1 to 2.3 and §4 read): fully nonlinear 2D simulations and a tank. Incipient breaker profile: steepness 0.335, Sk = 1.15, As = −0.51; experimental breakers "up to As = −0.5 instantaneously in Caulliez 2002 or As = −0.2 on average in Young and Babanin 2006" (second-hand for Caulliez 2002). In the simulations asymmetry oscillates ±0.45 through the modulation cycle, and wind changes the breaker's shape only at extreme forcing U/c = 11. Lab breakers: As −0.33 to +0.75 (20 highest waves).

### Norwegian shelf buoys [KM80]

Read in full; Tables 1 and 2 are unreadable in the scan. Data: Waverider buoys at Tromsøflaket, Halten and Utsira, 1974 to 1978, 20 min every 3 h, storms with Hs > 4 m; 1 754 waves with H > 5 m for the shape distributions, 6 353 for joint distributions. ε and λ follow Rayleigh-type distributions (correlation 0.99 and 0.94), μ a Gaussian (0.999). Maximum values over field and lab: λ = 7.7, ε = 0.83, μ = 0.92. The data were from surface-following buoys. Whether a buoy record keeps the front-back part of a wind-shifted harmonic was not found in any source. To second order the Lagrangian correction removes only the Stokes part, a cos 2θ term; the quadrature part a2 sin β sin 2θ would remain (derived here, not checked against a source).

### Myrhaug and Kjeldsen 1986, Guedes Soares et al. 2004

Not reached: publisher pages refuse automated access and no author copy was found. Their means of ε, λ, μ for North Sea or Norwegian storm waves are therefore not in this note.

### Slope skewness [GU23, CG12, LH82, CM54]

[GU23] retrieves the full slope pdf from IASI infrared glint, open ocean, wind 3 to 15 m/s; its Table 1 is reproduced in the findings table. The skewness coefficients agree with Cox and Munk's within CM's uncertainty and support Bréon and Henriot's non-linear wind dependence; the upwind/downwind pdf difference changes sign between 5 and 6 m/s ("we have no explanation for this change"). The axis convention (x "upwind") could not be tied unambiguously to front versus back faces from what was read, so this note reports magnitudes only. These statistics are dominated by centimetre-to-decimetre waves, not by the dominant waves.

Cox and Munk's linear fits (c21 = 0.01 − 0.0086 W, c03 = 0.04 − 0.033 W, W at 12.5 m) are known but were not read: the Yale archive and the Ifremer mirror refused access. Longuet-Higgins 1982 was not reached; per a search-engine summary of its abstract it ties the glitter offset to slope skewness, rules out damped single waves and gets the observed magnitude from short waves riding on longer ones (second-hand, not read).

[CG12] (tank, Marseille-Luminy, wind 3 to 10 m/s, fetch 2 to 26 m) gives the short-wave end: dominant wavelengths 3, 6, 14, 23 cm have elevation skewness 0.06, 0.38, 0.42, 0.43 and along-wind slope skewness of magnitude 0.4, 0.8, 0.6, 0.3 (slopes under 40°), negative, "the horizontal asymmetry of the wave profiles, which are bent in the wind direction" (Table 1, §5).

## 2. Which waves carry the lean in a young fetch-limited sea

Forcing at the owner's peak: u*/c_p = 0.044 to 0.061 [M]. Every line of evidence puts the average dominant wave at zero lean:

- Theory [ZF20 eq. 4.5, 5.6]: β = −0.011° to −0.021° at the peak [M]. Even a factor 10 on the growth conversion leaves it under 0.25°. For u*/c ≤ 0.09, LES puts the pressure maximum at ψ_P ≈ π, where the first-order β is zero [ZF20 §5.2].
- Tank [LE95, via the earlier note and ZF20 Fig. 8]: −A = −0.066 + 0.323 u*/C_m, zero at u*/C_m ≈ 0.20.
- Field: non-breaking waves symmetric (−0.017) even at u*/c_p ≈ 0.28 [YB06]; average storm waves symmetric [ST03, and Gullfaks and Tern second-hand].

What does lean:

- **The largest waves of a group**, by Ac −0.03 at 1.5 H̄ to −0.08 at 3.5 H̄, crest fronts 6 to 17% steeper than backs [ST03]. The paper does not separate breaking from non-breaking waves, and second-order random theory gives zero here, so this is beyond bound-wave theory.
- **Breaking crests**, −0.19 as a segment average [YB06], individual breakers to about −0.5 [BA10].
- **Short waves under strong relative forcing.** Thresholds from the tank fit (u*/c ≈ 0.2) mapped onto 6 to 12 m/s [M]:

| u*/c | Leykin A | ZF β (deep, ψ_P = 3π/4) | wavelength at 6 m/s | at 12 m/s |
| ---- | -------- | ----------------------- | ------------------- | --------- |
| 0.2  | 0.00     | −0.2°                   | 0.69 m              | 2.9 m     |
| 0.3  | −0.03    | −0.5°                   | 0.31 m              | 1.3 m     |
| 0.5  | −0.10    | −1.4°                   | 0.11 m              | 0.47 m    |
| 1.0  | −0.26    | −5.5°                   | 3 cm                | 12 cm     |

Leykin's fit was made for the dominant waves of a tank sea at steepness ≈ 0.165, not for short waves riding on long ones, so applying it to the tail is an extrapolation. The theory column is ten times smaller than the tank biphase at the same u*/c, which ZF20 attribute to the uncertain u*/c to growth conversion and to breaking.

Threshold summary: in wave age, none for dominant waves within the owner's range (they are all far below u*/c 0.2); in forcing, u*/c ≈ 0.2 for wind-driven lean of a given wave; in steepness, breaking (Babanin's limiting steepness, BA10) is what produces the large values.

## 3. Vertical asymmetry in young steep seas

[ZG21] (Black Sea platform, 30 m, resistive string gauge on a 6 m boom, 20-min records, June and December 2018 and January 2019, U10 0 to 26 m/s, wave age U10/c_p 0 to 5.2, steepness s = σ k_p 0.005 to 0.095; the text extraction garbles the steepness symbol, but λ3 = 3s for second order fixes s = σk_p): wind sea λ3 = 0.050 + 1.795 s (SD 0.052), swell λ3 = 0.016 + 2.547 s. Kurtosis excess −0.4 to 0.4, nearly uncorrelated with steepness. The second-order 3s rises faster than measured wind-sea skewness and leaves the one-SD band above s = 0.08. Ekofisk shows −0.05 to 0.4 (second-hand in ZG21).

Applied [M]:

| Sea                                                  | s = σk_p       | λ3 from ZG21 fit | 3s           |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| Helsinki mean, 0.84 m, 4.9 s                         | 0.035          | 0.11             | 0.11         |
| Archipelago breeze, 0.47 m, 3.0 s                    | 0.053          | 0.14             | 0.16         |
| Gulf fresh breeze, 2.1 m, 6.0 s                      | 0.059          | 0.155            | 0.18         |
| Wave-age route, c_p/U 0.58 to 0.78 (s = 0.021 U/c_p) | 0.027 to 0.036 | 0.10 to 0.12     | 0.08 to 0.11 |

So young Baltic-like seas have the storm skewness (0.10 to 0.16), not more, with a scatter of ±0.05 between records. Higher values come with breaking (0.45 in breaking segments, [YB06]) or shallow water. In Southern Ocean stereo data, young seas (c_p/U ≈ 0.5, k_p Hs/2 > 0.08) reached kurtosis 3.4 and crests above the Tayfun bound-wave distribution; skewness was not reported [TO24].

## 4. Other features of a young sea, briefly

- **Whitecaps on crests.** [DU21]: see the findings table. The breaking that makes whitecaps is on waves longer than 1 m (c > 1.25 m/s), and its maximum sits 15° on the upwind (back) side of the long-wave crest.
- **Short-wave shape.** Waves under ~10 cm: round bulge at the crest front with parasitic capillaries ahead; 10 to 20 cm: same with micro-breaking on the forward face; > 20 cm: Stokes-like pyramidal crests and flat troughs, micro-breaking on the highest crests of groups, plunging micro-breakers above 7 m/s [CG12].
- **Short waves gather on the long-wave crests**, which raises slope skewness [CG12 §2, citing Hung and Tsai, second-hand]; no field modulation transfer function for slope or roughness was read beyond the whitecap MTF above.
- **Spatial patchiness** of skewness and asymmetry over a few wavelengths [AG05].
- Crest length and spreading: earlier note §3. Wind drift: not researched here.

## Reproducing the computations

- _*u*/c and β._* u* = √Cd U10 with Cd = 1.2 × 10⁻³ up to 11 m/s and (0.49 + 0.065 U10) × 10⁻³ above (an assumed drag law). Pk/ρ_w g = 32.5 (ρ_a/ρ_w)(u*/c)² / sin ψ_P with ρ_a/ρ_w = 1.225 × 10⁻³ and ψ_P = 3π/4, then β = (Pk/ρ_w g)(sin 2ψ_P − sin ψ_P) at kh → ∞. Wavelength at a given u*/c from c = u*/(u*/c), λ = 2πc²/g.
- **Lake George record.** Finite-depth dispersion iterated at f = 0.36 Hz, h = 1.1 m.
- **Skewness of the owner's seas.** k_p = 4π²/(g Tp²), s = (Hs/4) k_p, λ3 = 0.050 + 1.795 s.
- **Stansell conversion.** λ = T4/T3 = (1 − Ac)/(1 + Ac).
- **Single-wave lean.** η(θ) = cos θ + (ε/2) cos(2θ + β) on 20 000 points; ratio of the largest downward slope ahead of the crest to the largest upward slope behind it; crest position by argmax.

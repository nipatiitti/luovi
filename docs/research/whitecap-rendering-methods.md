# Whitecap rendering methods: what makes foam read as thin crest streaks

Research note, 2026-10-24. Question: how published and shipped ocean renderers get whitecaps that read as thin, sharp-edged, crest-aligned streaks at the right count rather than soft blobs, and what that suggests for our setup (ADR 0011 as amended by #40: Dupuy and Bruneton's per-pixel Gaussian tail of the linearised breaking Jacobian, threshold solved for Brumer's W, breaking cutoff on the Jacobian channels, no persistence). Facts, derivations and options only; decisions belong to a design ticket. Builds on `research/foam-rework` (`docs/research/foam-rework.md`, #39).

Tags: **[S]** read first-hand in the cited source; **[2nd]** reached only through a summary or a citing paper; **[D]** derived here (method in the section named).

## What the benchmark photos and our renders show

- Real whitecaps (benchmark photos 2 to 4): many small white marks, each elongated along a crest and narrow across it, sitting on the forward face of crests of many scales, with hard edges; far away they become dots and dashes. Close up (photo 3) the residual foam is a torn lacework, not a fill.
- Our storm render with a 3.99 m breaking cutoff: few large, rounded, soft-edged patches several metres across. With a 0.14 m cutoff: isotropic speckle over the whole near field. Neither is crest-aligned. The 300 m view reads as uniform salt.

Donelan-Banner sech² spreading has β ≈ 1.24 at 1.6 ω_p, 0.96 at 2 ω_p, 0.69 at 3 ω_p; the aspect ratio is 1.32 at β = 1 and 1.10 at β = 0.7 [D, §B]. Our 3.99 m cutoff also removes the short waves that would break the blobs' edges, so the step is smooth and the patch is the size of the shortest kept wave. |
| 4   | Where sharpness comes from in shipped engines | **From thresholding a texture or a dither against the foam amount, not from the wave field.** Crest: `smoothstep(1 - foam, 1 - foam + feather, tex)` with feather 0.4 [CR `OceanFoam.hlsl`]. HDRP: "foam erosion" of a four-channel texture (micro and macro distance fields, temporal noise, foam noise), sharpness rising from 0.1 to 5 as foam ages [HD `FoamUtilities.hlsl`]. Atlas and Sea of Thieves: buffer "modulates" or is "blended with" an authored foam texture [AT; SOT]. Tessendorf et al. describe using the decaying map as opacity to "pull through" a separately authored pattern as common practice [T23 §2]. Yingst et al.: a halftone (dither) mask thresholded against the foam value, so bubbles pop as the value decays while clumping [YI11]. **[D, §C]** Thresholding a field U that is uniform on [0, 1] and independent of the foam value F gives $E[\mathbf 1\{U>1-F\}]=F$: a dither preserves mean coverage exactly while making every edge hard.                                                                                                       |
| 5   | Where crest alignment comes from              | **Four mechanisms, only one of which our build has.** (a) _Phase of a wave train_: foam peaks at the crest and decays behind it, spray occupies a plateau ahead of it whose width grows with dissipated energy, with sharp on and off edges [FO26 §9.3]; WaveWorks shoreline breakers use a narrow sawtooth in Gerstner phase with a delayed trail [WW]. (b) _Eigenvector of the Jacobian_: the minimum eigenvector is the direction the wave folds, the folded wave aligns with it [T04 §4]. (c) _Lagrangian compression_: foam evaluated at the rest position and drawn at the displaced one shrinks across the crest by the local Jacobian; Dupuy and Bruneton's look rests on λ up to 2.3 with ε near 0 to 0.2, where that compression is near total [foam-rework finding 3]. Our build has this at λ = 1 and ε ≈ 0.5, a factor of about two. (d) _Persistence on a fixed grid_: a crest moving through a decaying buffer leaves a trail behind it [AT, CR, SOT, T23, BL].                                                                                                         |

[CH17] Gaussian tail of $a_z$ against a Monahan-type law, plus a lifetime shape fitted by genetic algorithm. [T23] the reset-and-decay map has ensemble mean $\langle W\rangle=\langle\Theta\rangle/(1-\alpha(1-\langle\Theta\rangle))$ (eq. 11) and the threshold comes from the inverted empirical CDF of λ_min (eq. 13); α = e^{−0.15} is fitted, not derived (Algorithm 1). | 7   | Does our coverage solve count the right area  | **No: it counts rest-plane area, and the drawn foam covers about half of it where displacement is carried.** A rest area dA is drawn over J dA. The drawn coverage is $E[J\,\mathbf 1\{J<\epsilon\}]=P(J<\epsilon)\,(1-\sigma\varphi(u)/\Phi(u))$, $u=(\epsilon-1)/\sigma$. On the reference seas this is 0.20% against the 0.49% target (light), 0.63% against 1.35% (moderate), 1.96% against 3.70% (storm) [D, §B]. ## A. Linearised Jacobian and vertical acceleration

For one deep-water component $\eta=a\cos\theta$, $\theta=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-\omega t$, Tessendorf's displacement is $\mathbf D=-a\hat{\mathbf k}\sin\theta$ (the sign that compresses crests; our `wave_divergence` uses $-d\,k^2\cos\theta$ with $d=\lambda a/k$, `shading.ts`). Then $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=-ak\cos\theta=-k\eta$ and $a_z=\partial_t^2\eta=-\omega^2\eta=-gk\eta$. Both are linear, so the sum over the band and every cascade keeps $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=a_z/g$. With finite depth, $\omega^2=gk\tanh kh$ and $a_z=-gk\tanh(kh)\,\eta$. The breaking cutoff weight applies to both sides alike.

Two consequences. The criterion is not the problem (finding 2). And Tessendorf's displacement $\mathbf D$ is, up to sign and λ, the Riesz transform of η, $\hat{R}_j=-ik_j/|k|\,\hat\eta$, so $(\eta,\mathbf D)$ per cascade is the monogenic signal of the cascade's height [FS01]: local amplitude $A=\sqrt{\eta^2+|\mathbf D|^2}$, local phase $\operatorname{atan2}(|\mathbf D|,\eta)$, local orientation $\mathbf D/|\mathbf D|$. Felsberg and Sommer state this is exact for locally one-dimensional structure and needs extension for general 2D signals [FS01]. Our cascades already store η and D, so a per-cascade crest phase costs no new FFT.

## C. Mean-preserving dither

If U is uniform on [0, 1] and independent of F, $P(U>1-F)=F$. A dither therefore turns a smooth per-pixel coverage into hard-edged marks with the same mean, which is why the engines' texture thresholds do not change apparent coverage much when the texture histogram is flat. Crest's `smoothstep` with a 0.4 feather is a softened version of the same test. A physical choice of U already exists in our sea: the waves below the breaking cutoff, uniformised by their own Gaussian CDF, $U=\Phi((J_{short}-1)/\sigma_{short})$, which is independent of the long-wave Jacobian to the extent the cascades are uncorrelated, as [DB12 eq. 7] already assumes. Where the pixel footprint exceeds those waves, the dithered indicator aliases and the pixel should take the expectation F, the same footprint blend Atlas uses for its variance fix [AT, "Final tweaks"].

## What this suggests for our setup (options, not decisions)

1. **Per-cascade monogenic crest mask.** For each displacing cascade, local amplitude $A_c$ and signed phase from (η, D) already in its displacement texture; whitecap where the local steepness $\bar k_cA_c$ exceeds a threshold and the phase lies in a window on the forward face whose width is the observed 0.028 λ (about 0.18 rad) [KM23]. Along-crest length comes from the envelope staying above threshold, so the mark is short and crest-aligned instead of round. Coverage for a narrow band: $A^2$ is exponential with mean $2m_{0,c}$, phase uniform, so $W_c=\exp(-s^2/(2\bar k_c^2m_{0,c}))\cdot0.028$ and one s solves $\sum_cW_c=W$ in `sim-ocean` without render settings beyond the cut. Caveats: a cascade spans a decade, not a narrow band, and broad spreading breaks the 1D assumption [FS01], so the realised coverage must be checked by a scenario; the mask is non-linear and needs a footprint fallback (mip the per-texel mask, whose mean is exact, or fall back to [DB12]). Closest prior art: [FO26] per wave train.
2. **Dither the existing coverage with the sub-cutoff waves (section C).** Keeps the solved W in expectation, gives hard edges and a torn interior close up, costs one more texture read already in the shader, needs no new constant. It does not change the patches' outline, so it fixes softness, not shape.
3. **Count the drawn area (finding 7) and test λ_min (findings 1, 5b).** Solving with $E[J\,\mathbf 1\{J<\epsilon\}]$ instead of $P(J<\epsilon)$ roughly doubles the drawn coverage to the target where displacement is carried. λ_min needs the cross derivative ∂D_x/∂z per cascade (one more channel) and has no closed form; its threshold can come from the jointly Gaussian derivatives' covariance at build, as [T23] does from a histogram.

Persistence (trails, finding 5d) stays blocked on a cited active fraction as recorded in ADR 0011's amendment; [T23] eq. 11 is the closed-form steady state such a buffer would need, but its α is fitted.

# H-boat trim and behaviour reference (from the Finnish hWiki)

Source: the 19 pages of the Finnish H-boat wiki's trim category (Luokka:Trimmaus) plus linked pages, read November 2026. Everything comes from the wiki unless marked "(not from the wiki)". Wind is in m/s as given, knots in brackets. The wiki is written mostly for cruisers: much qualitative cause and effect, few numbers, and no heel angles, pointing angles, target speeds, manoeuvre timings, broaching or tiller feel.

## What the simulator must get right

1. With a very loose rig (upper shrouds 23, lowers 12 on a Loos gauge) and a 205 to 215 kg crew, the boat "stays upright" up to about 8 to 9 m/s (15.6 to 17.5 kn); above that the crew weight runs out and heavier crews sail past. (Maston trimmaus)
2. With a baggy main and a tight rig, the same light crew could not keep the boat upright "already in medium wind". Main depth, not wind alone, sets the overpowering point. (Maston trimmaus)
3. Reefing the main by 1 m reduces its area by about 20%, but straightens (de-heels) the boat "considerably more" than 20%, because the pressure at the masthead is removed. Boat speed is "hardly" affected. (Reivaus)
4. Sailing on one sail only works because the boat is well balanced, but upwind the pointing angle (nousukulma) "drops clearly". (Reivaus)
5. Tightening the backstay (takastaagi, hekki) bends the mast top aft, tightens the forestay, flattens the upper main and reduces the heeling force; it bends the mast "considerably more" than prebend does. (Takastaagi, Maston trimmaus)
6. Mast rake (takanoja) of 5 to 10% of the main luff P = 8.70 m, that is about 40 to 80 cm, improves upwind performance but slows the boat downwind under spinnaker. (Maston trimmaus)
7. In fresh wind, the masthead falling off to leeward opens the main leech and reduces heeling force; tight lowers with loose uppers make the tip fall to leeward; the tip falling to windward is never good. (Maston trimmaus)
8. Moving the jib car aft tightens the foot and opens the leech; the resulting twist reduces heel and "to some extent" the rocking (keikkuminen) in waves. Best upwind setting lies between the middle and aft positions. (Fokka)
9. Dropping the traveller (levanki) to leeward corrects heel and weather helm (the page says the boat is "luovikärkäs", eager to luff); on a reach, unstable helm balance (pinnan tasapaino) is eased by adding twist. (Levanki)
10. With the Code 0 (about 20 m2) in very light air below 5 m/s (9.7 kn), the boat makes "easily" 4 to 5 knots (2.1 to 2.6 m/s); the maker's maximum apparent wind for it is 7 m/s (13.6 kn). (Code 0 ja genaakkeri)

## Boat data (H-vene page)

Length 8.28 m, waterline 6.30 m, beam 2.18 m, draft 1.30 m, wetted surface 11.93 m2, minimum weight 1450 kg, keel 725 kg, mast 10.4 m, main 14.8 m2, jib 10.2 m2, spinnaker 36.0 m2, LYS 1.07. Racing crew 2 to 4, best 3 (Kilpaileminen). The jib clew reaches clearly aft of the mast (Fokka). The rig is fractional (osatakila) with one pair of swept-back spreaders (saalingit), chainplates aft of the mast, uppers (ylävantit) and lowers (alavantit); the backstay shapes the mast but is not needed to hold it up. Shrouds are 4 mm wire (Vantit ja vanttiruuvit). Forestay 8060 mm on one boat, 8340 mm for Botnia rigs (Etustaagi).

## Each control and its cause and effect

**Mainsheet (skuutti, jalus).** Pulls the boom down and in. With the traveller car under the boom, nearly all the sheet force acts vertically, the main flattens strongly and the leech closes (Levanki, picture C).

**Traveller (levanki).** Moving the car sideways changes the ratio of vertical to horizontal sheet pull; the boom stays near the centreline and the traveller mainly sets leech tension. The more directly the car is under the boom, the less sheet force is "lost" sideways. Car to windward: less downward pull, boom rises, leech opens, head twists off, steadier in gusts. Car to leeward with sheet tight: flatter main, closed leech. Rikin huolto: down to leeward in harder wind, up to windward for twist so gusts pass over the top. Short, high travellers strengthen the effect.

**Backstay (takastaagi, hekki).** Tightening bends the mast top aft, increases the forward curve of the mid mast, flattens the cupped main, tightens the forestay, straightens the jib luff and flattens the jib. Geometry given: the boom end to masthead line (roughly the leech) keeps its length while mast bend tightens a line through mid sail, removing the round shape. Result: less heel, better speed. It is one of the most important hard weather controls, reducing heeling moment "significantly". Fully slack while tuning and in light wind. Nurmio straightened the forestay with it "according to wind and wave pressure" for pointing.

**Kicker or vang (kikkitalja, puomin alasvetäjä).** Flattens the main in strong wind, lets it round in light wind, and prevents accidental gybes in fresh downwind sailing (Rikin huolto). It also adds to the flattening done by sheet and backstay (Takastaagi, Levanki).

**Cunningham (kunnari).** Tightening evens out the fullness and moves the deepest point forward toward the mast, flattening the after part. Loose in light wind (some luff wrinkles, deepest point mid sail); tight in fresh wind, where flow is unobstructed and heeling force is "relatively weaker".

**Outhaul (alaliikin kireys).** Named only as one of the main controls and as a flattening aid together with kicker and sheet. No settings given.

**Topping lift (dirkki).** In light wind it can lift the boom to put depth in the main; in hard wind the boom is pulled down to flatten it. Kept slack while sailing.

**Jib sheet and car (skuutti, skuuttivaunu, skuuttikisko).** Telltales (virtauslangat) on both sides should stream: leeward fluttering means sheet too tight, windward fluttering too loose. The sheet line should point at mid luff. Car forward: closes leech, fuller foot, for light wind and broad reaching. Car middle: even pull on leech and foot, for reaching. Car aft: tight foot, open leech, twist, for rising wind upwind, with less heel and less rocking in waves.

**Jib halyard and forestay.** A coachroof tackle (magicbox) sets halyard tension; forestay tension comes from the backstay.

**Shrouds (vantit).** Uppers must be "sufficiently, surprisingly, really" tight; lowers looser than uppers. Tightening uppers makes the spreaders push the mid mast forward (prebend). Tightening lowers pulls the mid mast aft. Side shrouds set the mast's lateral flexibility: more give in light wind and waves, more support in hard wind (Rikin huolto). Upper-to-lower ratio decides which way the tip falls sideways. Overtightening dents the hull sides at the chainplates.

**Rake, prebend, spreaders.** More rake helps upwind and hurts downwind. Prebend flattens the main and opens its leech; the right amount depends on the luff curve the sailmaker cut. Prebend is largest at the spreaders. Longer and more swept spreaders increase forward mid mast bend. The mid mast may bend forward but "NEVER" aft; the mast must never lean forward. A loose rig lets the mast stand more upright on the run, making the rig "more effective". A mast bearing evenly on its whole foot "pumps" less in waves; slack shrouds let it pump vertically and damage the deck (Ostajan opas).

**Spinnaker (spinaakkeri), pole, guy, sheet.** Pole perpendicular to the apparent wind; sheet trimmed actively so the luff curls slightly; both clews at equal height; pole never touching the forestay. On the H-boat the jib stays up under spinnaker except in the very lightest winds. Barber haulers can replace the downhaul (alagaija).

**Crew weight and hiking straps (roikkumaremmit).** Straps let the crew sit with upper bodies outboard, which reduces heel and increases speed. Crew weight changes the right rig: instructions developed with a 300 kg crew do not work for a 205 to 215 kg crew. A heavy outboard sinks the stern and makes the boat drag (Ostajan opas).

## Manoeuvres

The boat is "very agile": the fin keel (eväköli) and spade rudder turn her almost on the spot, unlike long-keeled boats, and hull windage is small. The rudder works only with flow past it and steers the opposite way going astern. The rudder was enlarged in 1977 and deepened 15 cm by Elvström; older small rudders may be a safety concern.

Heaving to (fokan pakkaaminen): back the jib, lock the tiller to windward; she stops, falls off, gathers way, the rudder bites and she comes up again.

Under motor she is turned on the spot by alternating short kicks of reverse and forward thrust with the outboard swung to opposite extremes, at low speed.

A single line quick reef takes "roughly a minute"; slack reef lines leave a bag that catches wind.

Spinnaker: hoist from the companionway, usually to leeward in the main's lee; douse by releasing the guy, easing the halyard and pulling in by the sheet, easiest near dead downwind. Gybe end for end with the spinnaker centred, both barbers half on and the downhaul slightly eased; two crew can do it since the sheets need no trim during the gybe. An unfurled Code 0 must gybe round the front of the forestay.

No page describes tacking technique, tacking timing, roll tacking, or speed loss in tacks.

## Disagreements and gaps

**Shroud tension.** North tightens with wind, 31/25 to 41/38; Nurmio never dared reach the hard setting because the hull sides deformed. Collberg, for Ottosson sails, said very loose in all winds, and Nurmio found speed at 23/12. The difference is tied to sail cut and crew weight.

**Negative prebend.** Lowers tighter than uppers is reported as a hard weather trim "said to be used by some top sailors". It failed for a light crew because the main could not be flattened enough.

**Fore and aft mast shape.** The main text says the mid mast may bend forward but never aft, while the test sail paragraph says the mast should be "evenly bent backward" (tasaisesti taaksepäin taipunut) lengthwise. These may describe the same curve (top aft, middle forward) but the wording conflicts.

**Traveller.** Levanki says car to leeward in fresh wind "closes the leech", yet also that lowering it cures heel and weather helm, and that raising it to windward on a reach adds twist. The last is the reverse of usual practice (not from the wiki), possibly a wording error.

**Measured versus folk wisdom.** Gauge numbers, the 8 to 9 m/s limit and the 4 mm per 2 m result come from named crews. The rake range is hedged with "lienee" (presumably). "Reefing hardly affects speed", "twist reduces rocking" and the Code 0 speed are experience, not measurement.

**Gaps.** No heel angles, jib change winds, target speeds or angles, weather helm or tiller load figures, manoeuvre timing, broaching, or chop technique beyond twist and a flexible rig. No numbers for mast position or pole height. Many pages point to forum threads (hFoorumi, such as "Kovan tuulen kryssi") that are outside the wiki and were not read.

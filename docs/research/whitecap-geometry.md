# Whitecap geometry: counts, sizes, shapes and placement to judge a frame against

Research note, 2026-10-24. Question: what is the measured geometry of real whitecaps as a function of wind speed and wave age (number per area, size distribution, crest length per area from Λ(c), aspect ratio, where the white sits on the wave and how far down the face, active lifetime and residual fading, onset of wind streaks and spindrift, active against residual coverage), and what targets that gives for the three reference seas (light 8 m/s fetch 50 km, moderate 12 m/s fetch 150 km, storm 20 m/s fetch 300 km). Motivation: our rendered whitecaps are large soft blobs, while the owner's benchmark photos show thin, sharp, crest-aligned streaks and dashes. Facts and targets only; the breaking cutoff wavelength is settled elsewhere (`research/breaking-cutoff`) and only used here as an input.

Terms: **length** L is the whitecap's extent along the crest (its major axis), **width** l its extent across the crest in the direction of travel (minor axis), **aspect** L/l. λ_b, c and T_b are the wavelength, phase speed and period of the wave that breaks; λ_p, c_p of the spectral peak. Active (stage A) foam is the growing patch carried by the breaking crest; residual (stage B) foam is what it leaves behind.

| 6   | Active life, then residual       | **Active white lasts tenths of a second to about two seconds; residual foam lasts seconds and changes shape.** The active lifetime is exponentially distributed with mean 0.46 s; τ/T_b averages 0.3, lies in 0.1 to 0.41 with 75% probability, and does not depend on wind [KMDK22 §3.1]. During the active phase the surface behind the whitecap is free of residual foam; afterwards residual spots separate, the patch stops advancing with the crest and its centroid oscillates with the orbital motion, and it becomes a scattered spotty structure [KM23 Fig. 2, 3]. Reflectance falls from 20 to 55% at breaking to 3 to 10% after 10 s (Koepke 1984 via [DI19], read in `research/foam-rework`).                            |

What a frame should show, in plain terms: at 12 m/s a 25 m by 40 m patch of sea holds one or two actively breaking crests at any instant, each a white dash under a metre to a few metres long, a third as wide, lying along its crest and crosswind, sharp-edged, sitting on the crests of the longer waves and slightly behind them, with fainter broken spots trailing where earlier ones were. At 20 m/s the same patch holds around eight, a few several metres long, and foam begins to stretch into streaks along the wind. Read against this, the step 1 and prototype frames (`storm.3.99.png`, `moderate.1.png`, `light.1.png`, `storm-300m.1.png`, in the session scratchpad and not measured) show near-field patches of tens of square metres with aspect near 1 to 2, soft edges and no crosswind elongation, and at range a dense speckle; both depart from every row above except total coverage.

## Measuring a frame against the targets

The field methods that produced these numbers can be applied to a rendered frame without new theory:

- Rectify to the sea plane (the build knows the camera), threshold the foam channel, and take connected components; fit each with an ellipse to get L, l, orientation and area, as [KMK18], [KM23] and [ST17] do.
- Separate active from residual by frame differencing: a pixel that turned white within the last few tenths of a second and moves with the crest is active [ST17 §4b, the Schwendeman and Thomson 2015 "pixel flip"; KM11 §4].
- Count components per 1000 m² of rectified area, histogram L, and report median, 90th percentile and aspect; compare with the table. Keep only components whose whole outline is inside the window, as the Black Sea data did, or the size distribution will not be comparable.

## 1. Number per area and crest length per area

### 1.1 Direct counts

[KMK18] counted active breakers in a trapezoidal window with bases of 14 to 16 m and 29 to 48 m (a frame of about 400 m² in the same set-up, [KMDK22 §2.2]) from 11.4 m height at 1 to 2.5 cm resolution, 15 records at 10 to 16 m/s and wave ages 0.3 to 0.8, keeping breakers faster than 2.5 m/s, the speed above which detection was reliable. The instantaneous number per unit area rises from about $1\times10^{-3}$ to $3\times10^{-3}$ m⁻² over $u__$ of 0.38 to 0.65 m/s, fitted by $n=0.0128u__^{2.5}$ [Fig. 7]. A Phillips-type model, number per area $\propto k\Lambda(k)$ with Λ from the wind input and the measured spectrum, reproduces the slope ($u_*^{2.4}$) but needs its level set by hand [eq. 3 to 10].

With Large and Pond's drag coefficient, $u__$ is 0.28, 0.43 and 0.85 m/s on the reference seas, so n is 0.52, 1.53 and 8.4 per 1000 m². Only the moderate sea lies inside the measured range. How [KMK18] computed $u__$ is not stated; the Black Sea fetch and swell differ from the reference seas.

Phillips's relations tie Λ to the other targets: $\int c\Lambda\,dc$ is the fraction of surface swept by breaking fronts per second, and coverage is $\int c\,\tau\,\Lambda\,dc$ with τ the time foam stays [KM10 eq. 15, 16]. GOTEX's active coverage matches its first moment with a mean breaking duration of 2 s, which Kleiss and Melville read as the ensemble aspect ratio of crest length to foam patch width rather than an observed duration [KM10 §4d]. The Black Sea data fit $W_E=0.11\cdot\tfrac{2\pi\gamma_\tau}{g}\int c^2\Lambda\,dc$ with $\gamma_\tau=0.33$, the factor 0.11 explained by the patch width growing at 0.2c rather than c [KM23 eq. 7, Appendix].

### 2.2 Size targets, lower bound

A truncated power law $p(L)\propto L^{-3.23}$ on $[L_{min},L_{max}]$ with $L_{min}=0.2\lambda(2\text{ m/s})=0.51$ m (the breaking cutoff wave) and $L_{max}=0.2\lambda(0.5c_p)$ gives:

| Sea      | $L_{max}$ | Median $L_m$ | 90th percentile | Mean   | Median width at 0.41 |
| -------- | --------- | ------------ | --------------- | ------ | -------------------- |
| light    | 1.55 m    | 0.67 m       | 1.12 m          | 0.76 m | 0.28 m               |
| moderate | 4.22 m    | 0.70 m       | 1.39 m          | 0.87 m | 0.29 m               |
| storm    | 9.44 m    | 0.70 m       | 1.43 m          | 0.90 m | 0.29 m               |

The means sit on [KMK18]'s measured 0.69, 0.79 and (extrapolated) 1.01 m, which supports the floor. The median barely moves with wind because a steep power law is set by its floor; only the tail grows. These sizes are a lower bound for what a viewer sees: the window kept only whitecaps wholly inside a few hundred square metres, and they describe the growing crest only.

Consistency check: n times the mean time-averaged active area ($S_m/3$, [KM23 Appendix]) gives 0.003%, 0.016% and 0.11% coverage, matching the Black Sea's strict growth-phase coverage (up to 0.18%, [KM23 Fig. 8]) but a seventh to a tenth of Scanlon and Ward's W_A. Dividing Scanlon and Ward's W_A by n instead gives a mean active patch of 0.6 to 1 m² per whitecap, which matches [CA24]'s field peak areas (next section).

### 2.3 Size targets, upper bound

[CA24]'s 508 tracked whitecaps (camera 23 m up, 59° from nadir, 3 to 6 frames per second, winds 5.7 to 13.7 m/s, mixed swell) have peak total foam areas $A_o$ of 0.2 to 26 m². Read from Fig. 7a: modes near 0.8 to 1 m² at 5.7 and 8.2 m/s, 1.5 m² at 13.7 m/s and 2 m² at 11.4 m/s; on a log axis the curves fall to half height about a factor of 3 either side, so the 90th percentile is about 3 times the median (our reading). Through $S=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\cdot0.41L^2$, area 1 m² is L = 1.8 m, l = 0.72 m; 1.7 m² is L = 2.3 m, l = 0.94 m; three times those areas give L of 3.1 and 4.0 m. No field size distribution at 20 m/s was found.

The two bounds differ because they measure different things: the growing active crest in a small window, against the peak of the whole visible patch including foam just shed. A frame shows both, so the targets give the range.

## 3. Where the white sits

- **On the breaking wave** [ST17]: 103 clear breakers from a ship in the North Pacific (U20 up to 23 m/s, Hs to 6 m). Whitecap pixels are up to ten times more likely than others at surface slopes near 1.25; at the break point the breaker is almost fully above the mean water line, and the slope maximum shifts from front face to back face through breaking. The white therefore starts at the crest and spreads forward with it, not down the trough.
- **How far down**: the active patch is 0.03 λ_b wide on average and grows to 0.06 to 0.08 λ_b, a twelfth to a sixth of the half wavelength between crest and trough (derived from [KM23]); behind it the surface stays free of residual foam until the active phase ends [KM23 §Experiment, Fig. 2].
- **On the long wave** [KMDK22, DU21]: Black Sea, 10 to 20 m/s, dominant waves of 32 to 69 m. Breakers start 9.6° ahead of the long-wave crest and coverage peaks 11.6° behind it [KMDK22], or 15° windward of it [DU21], independent of wind and steepness; whitecaps "disappear" in the troughs, a modulation transfer function of about 20 [DU21]. Whitecap coverage rises during wave-group passage by two to three times, active coverage by about five times [MA22 abstract].
- **Which waves break**: in developing seas breakers reach the peak phase speed; in developed seas they stay below half of it [KMK18]. Dominant-wave breaking supplies more than half the coverage when seas are young [PI21]. The reference seas have $c_p/U$ of 0.86 to 0.96, so they are treated as developed.

## 4. Life and fading

- Active: exponential lifetimes, mean 0.46 s at the Black Sea (video floor 0.12 s); radar and acoustic studies give 0.8 to 3 s with floors of 0.5 s [KMDK22 §1, §3.1]. Field time to peak area 0.3 to 3 s, mode about 1 s, without a clear wind trend [CA24 Fig. 7b].
- Transition: a mixed phase in which residual spots separate while the front still advances; the area stops growing at the end of the active phase [KM23 Fig. 2d, 3a].
- Residual: the patch stops moving with the crest, oscillates with the orbital velocity [KM23 Fig. 3c] and is advected as old foam [KM11 Fig. 4, patch A]; area decays as $c_0t^ne^{-c_1t}$ rather than a pure exponential, with surfactant-stabilised tails [CA24 eq. 3, Fig. 6]; whole lifetimes 2 to 20 s [CA24 §3.1].
- Streaks: Beaufort 7 and above [NOAA]. No source read gives a quantitative streak onset, spacing or length.

## Reproducing the numbers

All derived values come from the formulas above, evaluated in a throwaway Python script, not committed:

- Peak: $f_p=3.5(g/U)\chi^{-0.33}$, $\chi=gX/U^2$, deep water, $c_p=gT_p/2\pi$, $\lambda_p=gT_p^2/2\pi$, g = 9.80665 m/s².
- $u__=U\sqrt{C_D}$ with Large and Pond's $C_D$; counts $n=0.0128u__^{2.5}$ m⁻².
- Coverage: Brumer $0.0738(U-4.23)^{1.42}$ percent; Scanlon and Ward $7.84\times10^{-6}(U-2.56)^3$ and $1.39\times10^{-6}(U-1.80)^3$ as fractions.
- Crest length: $3.3\times10^{-4}(U/10)^3e^{-0.64c_0}/0.64$ m⁻¹ above $c_0$.
- Sizes: $\lambda=2\pi c^2/g$, $L_m=0.2\lambda$; truncated power law quantile $L_q=L_{min}\,[1-q(1-(L_{max}/L_{min})^{-2.23})]^{-1/2.23}$; moments by direct integration; ellipse $S=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\,0.41\,L^2$.
- Active life $0.3\cdot2\pi c/g$ at c = 2 m/s and c = 0.5 c_p.

# Side force is lift plus cross-flow at every angle, solved by basis superposition over heel bins

The hull chapter switches between a vortex-lattice lift model and a cross-flow drag model by drift angle and speed, mirrors the lattice with an image that blends from rigid wall to anti-image with Froude number, and solves a matrix-vector product per tick. Every unified model in the yacht and ship manoeuvring literature instead sums the two terms at every angle, and the one source that analyses both image rules rejects both. We decided: lift and cross-flow are summed, with a stall weight per lifting surface on its local inflow angle (stall onset and end angles per surface, by aspect ratio) and no speed cut-off; beyond stall a cited appendage polar carries a fin's force; a rigid-wall image at every Froude number with Slooff's pressure relief per strip (κ 1.5) replaces the Froude blend; a fin's lattice is carried over through the canoe body to the waterline on the heeled mid-surface of each heel bin (10° bins, 0 to 60°, mirrored), with a second lattice per bin whose Kutta condition sits on the leading edge for reversed inflow; the turning rudder rotates its panel normals in the right-hand side; the per-tick force is a weighted sum of about thirty basis solutions per bin (six rigid-body velocities, twelve for the rudder's cos δ and sin δ, twelve for a uniform-plus-gradient orbital field), precomputed at load from an inverse that is then dropped, with no bake cache and nothing in the contract; a two-state Wagner lag per surface (Küssner for the orbital part) scaled by A/(1+A) is integrator state. The VLM owns induced drag, the lift part of rotational damping and the linear manoeuvring derivatives, which are derived from the basis at load; the rigid body's 6x6 added mass with its full Kirchhoff terms owns the Munk moment; the hydro block's damping fields hold only the non-lifting remainder and its derivatives field is optional.

Decided by the VLM lattice, heel bins, inverse strategy and cross-flow blend ticket ([#16](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/16)) on 2026-10-21, from the side-force architecture research ([#17](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/17)) and the cross-flow research ([#15](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/15)).

## Considered options

- Switching between lift and cross-flow (the chapter): needs a speed branch, cuts the H-boat keel's lift far too early (it keeps lifting beyond 30°, the rudder stalls near 20°) and contradicts every source read.
- Anti-image or a Froude blend towards it: forces circulation to zero at the surface and under-predicts side force; free-surface panels cost hundreds of panels for the same answer.
- Full matrix-vector product with a stored inverse: 0.36 ms of the hero boat's 1 ms at 600 panels and megabytes per bin; the right-hand side is linear in a few kinematic inputs, so the basis sum gives the same answer at a twentieth of the cost.
- Rudder-angle bins and a free wake: both multiply or rebuild the inverse for geometry that the stall weight discards anyway.
- A v_n² plate law for stalled fins: a quarter to a half of the measured normal force between stall and 40 to 55°, exactly where a failed tack or a broach sits.

## Consequences

- The ship state carries about twelve lag doubles per H-boat (two per surface per filter); per-surface force, local angle and stall weight are derived outputs, never state.
- The VLM settings block of the ship definition holds, per lifting surface, chordwise and spanwise panel counts, a carry-over count for fins, stall onset and end angles, the effective aspect-ratio root factor and the appendage polar by value; per ship, heel bin spacing and maximum, κ, and the wake vortex core radius as a chord fraction. A schema maximum of 800 panels per lattice bounds load time. Spacing rules and the Jones approximation constants of the Wagner and Küssner functions are algorithm constants in `sim-hull`, cited in place, under the ADR 0002 reading of the no-magic-coefficients rule.
- Cross-flow on the canoe body is the strip integral ½ρ·C_D(x)·T(x)·|v_n|·v_n over the union of hull-role and non-lifting appendage solids at the current attitude, with v_n including r·x and p·z; the diamond-section table and the finite-length factor 0.52 (0.63 recorded as the upper bound) are cited data in `data-hboat`, judged by the lying-ahull benchmark alone.
- Heel-induced side force at zero leeway comes from the heeled mid-surface geometry; its check against the Delft series heeled intercepts is outside this map, and the cited zero-lift leeway regression is the fallback if that check ever fails.
- The DSYHS regression is used without its leeway and induced terms; wave drag due to side force has no owner and graduates to a ticket only if the H-boat polar misses upwind.
- A `hullBody` lifting surface (Suomen Joutsen, step 6) still needs a relief chord and a Munk split of its own; neither is decided here.

# The CPU owns the sea below a cut wavenumber; the GPU renders the tail

The authoritative sea is a band of N equal-energy wave components below a cut wavenumber k_s, discretised off-lattice from the scenario's spectrum and seed in `sim-ocean`. The renderer draws those same components analytically and adds an FFT tail whose spectrum is zero below k_s, so nothing is counted twice and every wave a hull feels is on screen. The alternative the ocean chapter implied, a CPU subset of the FFT lattice, was rejected because the energy near the spectral peak is spread over hundreds of lattice bins: the 32 largest bins hold only 27 to 61% of the height variance in light and moderate Baltic seas, so a hull would float out of step with the swell drawn around it. Shrinking the tile until 32 bins hold the peak makes the swell repeat every few wavelengths.

Decided by the Ocean and environment query contract ticket ([#8](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/8)) on 2026-10-21, from the spectrum sampling research ([#4](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/4)) and the ocean wave survey ([#18](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/18)).

## Consequences

- The cut is one number shared by band and tail, carried by the scenario as a ratio k_s / k_p with default 3. The sea never depends on which ships sail it.
- Band amplitudes are fixed at sqrt(2 m0_band / N), not Rayleigh-distributed as Tucker et al. 1984 and Fréchot recommend, so the band's variance equals the spectrum's for every seed. The tail keeps Gaussian amplitudes with the corrected normalisation ½(ξr + iξi) sqrt(F Δkx Δky), never the reference shaders' sqrt(2 F Δk²), which is 8 times too much energy.
- Directional spreading is Donelan-Banner for both band and tail, because its cumulative inverts in closed form, which the equal-energy direction sectors need.
- Choppiness is clamped at build so the band's contraction constant K = λ Σ k_i A_i stays below 0.8; the band then never folds and two fixed-point iterations invert the displacement.
- The tail's initial spectrum is generated on the CPU with the seeded RNG and uploaded, so it is reproducible, but it is never authoritative and no force reads it.
- The band's wavenumbers and frequencies are global per scenario: no distance fade, no per-point depth, and the GPU never recomputes them.
- A spatially varying sea may later scale the band's amplitudes from authoritative data; it may never change the band's wavenumbers pointwise.

## Amendment, 2026-10-22

Amended by the design doc corrections task ([#25](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/25)) from the modern coefficient sources research ([#24](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/24)) and the render-ocean decision ([#23](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/23)).

- Donelan-Banner spreading takes the β exponent from Donelan, Hamilton and Hui 1985 inside 0.56 to 1.6 ω_p and floors it at 1.24 outside that range, the published high-frequency limit, so the band's direction sectors never collapse onto the mean direction. Banner 1990's upper branch is evaluated on k/k_p under TMA, not on ω/ω_p, because the TMA depth factor moves the frequency of a given wavenumber and the spreading was measured against wavenumber.
- Whitecap reflectance comes from Dierssen 2019 (CC BY), 0.40 for a fresh whitecap and 0.22 as the lifetime average, replacing Koepke 1984, which the research could not obtain and which the open literature reproduces.
- Fixed band amplitudes change the crest statistics. The sum of N fixed-amplitude cosines with random phases has lighter tails than a Gaussian sea: its highest crest is bounded by the sum of the amplitudes, and crests above about twice the significant height are rarer than the Rayleigh crest distribution of a Gaussian sea predicts. Hs is exact for every seed by construction; extreme-value statistics are not, and no benchmark depends on them. The tail keeps Gaussian amplitudes, so the short waves drawn on top of the band keep their natural crest statistics.

## Amendment, 2026-10-24

Decided by the wave shape ticket ([#43](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/43)) from the Baltic wave shape research (`research/baltic-wave-shape`) and the band size prototype (`prototype/band-size`, never merged), on which the owner judged the sea by screenshots and measured frame rate. It replaces N = 32, the 48 ceiling and the reference seas above; the rest stands.

- The band defaults to N = 128 components (32 strips by 4 sectors), with a ceiling of 128. At 32 the band's height visibly repeats: its self-correlation at shifts of 3 to 15 peak wavelengths is 0.45 with the tail drawn, falling as about 2.8/√N to 0.24 at 128. The 48 ceiling was set against the 1 ms budget that ADR 0013 removed. On an M1 Pro at 2560x1440 the sea runs at 114 to 120 fps from a 2 m eye, against 166 to 173 at N = 32.
- Height carries bound waves: the vertical second-order correction at each point's Lagrangian label, in the query and the band's vertex stage alike. The horizontal map and the inversion are unchanged and the tail stays linear. Without them choppy displacement leaves crests symmetric (skewness about 0.01, against 0.10 to 0.20 with them and 0.14 to 0.17 in field storms, Fedele et al. 2016). The form is the factorised ½(η·η_k + H·H_k), exact for collinear waves, times one constant solved at build as the ratio of the exact pair kernel's skewness weight to the factorised one's (0.69 to 0.74 on the reference seas). It lands within 2% of the exact pair sum's skewness and 0.01 Hs of its 0.1% crest, and needs only products of each component's cos and sin. The exact pair sum was rejected because its N(N+1)/2 pairs per vertex halved the frame rate already at N = 32. Published version: Guérin et al. 2019.
- The query evaluates each component's cos and sin once per point and rotates them for the two inverse-displacement passes by a polynomial in k·δx, multiplications only, agreeing with a fresh evaluation to 10⁻¹⁵ m. At N = 128 a boat of 200 query points costs 1.5 to 1.7 ms per tick on an M1 Pro, 9 to 11 boats per 60 Hz tick (5 to 6 without the rotation). Starting from the previous tick's labels was rejected: up to 1.7 mm off, and the answer would depend on history.
- The contraction clamp stays at K < 0.8. K grows as √N at fixed variance, so at N = 128 the effective λ is 0.66 to 0.75; the owner judged the look with that λ.
- The cut ratio default stays 3: a cut of 2 lowers the band's height self-correlation only from 0.45 to 0.41 and moves 9% of the height variance out of the sea a hull feels.
- The reference seas are three measured Baltic cases, each with its own seed. Archipelago: 8 m/s over 15 km, 22 m deep, γ 1 (Hs 0.47 m, Tp 3.0 s; Suomenlinna). Gulf: 12 m/s over 80 km, 60 m, γ 3.3 (2.1 m, 6.0 s; the Helsinki 95th percentile). Storm: 27 m/s over 194 km, 120 m (7.1 m, 10.5 s; the Bothnian Sea storm Aapeli, 8.1 m measured). They replace light, moderate and storm, all one open Baltic Proper geometry on seed 1, whose moderate sea was a 95th to 99th percentile Baltic sea. γ 1 stands in for the archipelago's plateau spectra, which no JONSWAP fits (Björkqvist et al. 2019).
- A quick-tier benchmark checks, on each reference sea, the band's realised height skewness on the grid against the exact second-order skewness computed headless within 10%, and the 0.1% crest within 0.02 Hs. Band Hs stays a check on the linear band.

## Amendment, 2026-11-15

Decided by the owner on the ocean update, from ten sailing seasons of Finnish Meteorological Institute buoy data (`research/baltic-sailing-season`), the wave asymmetry research (`research/wave-asymmetry`) and a prototype judged by screenshots. It withdraws the 2026-10-24 amendment's band size, bound waves, rotated query passes, reference seas and skewness benchmark, none of which was built; that amendment's cut ratio and contraction clamp stand, as does everything before it.

- The band stays at N = 32 components (8 strips by 4 sectors) and its height stays linear. The owner judged the 128-component band with bound waves on its prototype: heavy, with no notable difference in the picture.
- The frequency spectrum takes the measured equilibrium range above its peak. S is JONSWAP at the peak and, from 1.5 ω_p, the higher of JONSWAP and α g² ω⁻⁴ ω_p⁻¹ exp(−(ω_p/ω)⁴) with α = 0.006 (U/c_p)^0.55, the rear face Donelan, Hamilton and Hui 1985 measured for 1.5 < ω/ω_p < 3.5 (read from the 1983 manuscript, which notes that Kahma 1981's Gulf of Bothnia spectra agree). Beyond 3.5 ω_p the level reached falls as ω⁻⁵, since the law is not measured there. JONSWAP's ω⁻⁵ leaves the 1 to 3 m waves up to half the measured energy, and those waves are what a Baltic sea shows as chop. Band and tail read the one formula; the reference seas' Hs rises by 1 to 3%, because the range starts near the band's upper edge. A fully developed sea steps up by about 7% where the range starts, which no benchmark resolves.
- The reference seas are four measured Baltic cases, each with its own seed. Archipelago: 8 m/s over 15 km, 22 m deep, γ 1 (Hs 0.48 m, Tp 3.0 s; the Suomenlinna buoy's 90th percentile summer day). Sailing day: 7 m/s over 30 km, 60 m, γ 1 (0.63 m, 3.6 s; the Gulf of Finland buoy measures 3.9 s at that height in May to September). Gulf: 12 m/s over 115 km, 60 m, γ 1 (2.13 m, 6.7 s; a 2.1 m sea measures 6.7 s at the Gulf of Finland, Northern Baltic Proper and Bothnian Sea buoys, and is exceeded 1.5% of summer time in the gulf). Storm: 27 m/s over 194 km, 120 m, γ 3.3 (7.2 m, 10.5 s; the Bothnian Sea storm Aapeli, 8.1 m measured). They replace light, moderate and storm, whose moderate sea ran its crests at 22 kn where the summer median is 11 to 14 kn. The scenario tool opens on the sailing day.
- γ 1 on the three summer seas is a stand-in. Measured summer seas have a median Hs/Lp of 0.026, and the build's fetch laws cannot go below 0.033 at γ 3.3 or 0.027 at γ 1; a median day carries older, longer waves beside the wind sea, which one wave system per scenario cannot hold. That stays with the Coastal wave fields fog.
- The band gets no forward lean. Field data show none in dominant waves on average (Stansell, Wolfram and Zachary 2003: crest fronts 6 to 17% steeper than backs only in waves above 1.5 times the mean height; Young and Babanin 2006: asymmetry −0.017 in trains without breaking against −0.186 in breaking ones). A prototype of wind-driven lean on the tail's short waves, of the horizontal stretch in per-pixel normals and of long waves steepening the short ones showed no difference in the picture, so none is built. Lean of the steepest and breaking crests has no published model and waits on the owner's judgement of this sea.
- The band Hs and tail variance benchmarks are restated on the four seas, and the environment hash runs on the gulf.

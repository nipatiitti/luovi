# The sea is drawn from the band per vertex and a CPU-generated tail on a clipmap

The renderer draws the authoritative band by summing its N components in the vertex shader from the uploaded wave vectors, frequencies, amplitudes and per-frame phases, and draws the tail as three 256² FFT cascades whose initial spectrum a pure `sim-ocean` function generates on the main thread from the sea description and the renderer's own cascade layout. Both sit on a camera-centred clipmap whose rings double in spacing, snap to their own world grid and morph continuously in their outer quarter. A wave a ring cannot carry, band component or tail cascade alike, loses its vertex displacement over a smooth ramp and hands its slope variance to that ring's roughness; nothing fades for being far. The foam threshold is not tuned: at sea-state build it is solved from the spectrum's Jacobian statistics so the filtered far-field coverage matches a cited whitecap coverage law at the sea's generating wind.

The alternatives were a camera-centred band texture (rejected because hardware filtering would put an unreproducible error between the water a hull feels and the water drawn under it, and because 32 `sincos` per vertex is cheaper than the texture pass), the Worker generating the tail from a layout the host sends it (rejected because it couples the host protocol to render settings), a projected grid or quadtree mesh (rejected because vertices slide or pop on a displaced surface, and an infinite ocean gives a quadtree nothing to cull), a distance fade on the band (rejected because a far ship would float on water drawn differently from the water it feels) and an artist-chosen breaking threshold (rejected by the no-magic-coefficients rule).

Decided by the render-ocean v1 ticket ([#23](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/23)) on 2026-10-22, from the ocean wave survey ([#18](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/18)) and the ocean contract ([#8](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/8), `docs/adr/0005`).

The band is now 128 components (ADR 0005, amended 2026-10-24). Drawing them per vertex runs at 114 to 120 fps on an M1 Pro at 2560x1440; the comparison against a band texture was not re-measured at that size.

## Consequences

- The GPU receives the band's wave vectors, frequencies and amplitudes and never recomputes them; phases are reduced on the CPU in f64 at the published slot's tick time, with the camera origin's phase folded in, so the shader works on camera-relative positions in f32. The tail's time uniform is the same tick time; pause freezes the whole sea.
- Cascade tiles derive from the cut: cascade 0's tile is six cut wavelengths, cascade c holds wavelengths from a sixth of its tile down to a sixth of the next, the tile ratio is 10 and the last tile is floored at 1.28 m. A fourth cascade appears only when three cannot reach the floor. The tail's dispersion is gravity-only finite depth computed per lattice bin; its per-cascade choppiness defaults to the scenario's unclamped λ.
- Render settings (cascades, rings, foam buffer size) live in `render-ocean`, are overridable by the host and never enter the scenario or the state hash.
- Whether a wave displaces a vertex depends on the ring's spacing, not on distance: under four spacings per wavelength it ramps into roughness; a cascade displaces where its longest wave spans at least eight spacings.
- Roughness is per-cascade slope moments with mips and cross terms, plus the band's exact slope covariance, plus a cited floor equal to the Cox and Munk total slope variance minus the resolved variance.
- Foam near the camera is a density buffer with injection where the combined Jacobian falls below the threshold, advection by the surface velocity, exponential decay at a cited time and no diffusion; beyond it, Dupuy and Bruneton's filtered coverage from the same threshold. The two coverages differ slightly at the blend; that mismatch is judged by eye and has a trigger on the map.
- Every optical and foam constant (refractive index equation and Baltic inputs, water absorption, CDOM, foam reflectances, decay time, coverage law) lives in `data/src/shared/` with its citation; the renderer receives values.
- One quick-tier benchmark checks the tail generator's realised variance per cascade against the spectrum's integral over its annulus, and band plus tail against the full variance, within 5%. Nothing on the GPU is measured.

## Amendment, 2026-10-24

Decided by the Foam rework design ticket ([#40](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/40)), after the foam rework research ([#39](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/39), `research/foam-rework`) and the step 1 review ([#26](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/26)). It replaces the foam consequence above and the threshold's place in the opening paragraph; the rest stands.

- Whitecaps are one field at every distance: Dupuy and Bruneton's Gaussian tail of the linearised breaking Jacobian, evaluated per pixel in the surface fragment shader from the cascade textures at the mip the pixel's footprint selects, plus the band's divergence from the vertex stage. Up close the variance vanishes and coverage is a sharp step at the cascades' own resolution; far away it is the expected coverage. There is no near buffer for breaking, no blend ring and no per-texel band sum. A band wave a ring drops from displacement hands its divergence variance to that ring's σ², as it hands its slope variance to roughness. Step 1's near buffer filled by filtered injection is superseded: its 0.5 m texel hid the sharp field, and its persistence on top of a threshold solved for total coverage counted the decay twice (30 to 40% near against 3.7% far on the storm sea).
- Only waves at least as long as a cited breaking cutoff move the breaking Jacobian: a smooth spectral weight on the Jacobian channels before the inverse FFT, never on displacement or roughness. Centimetre waves carry a third to a half of the full Jacobian's variance and never entrain air, so without it foam marks ripple compressions instead of breaking crests. Dupuy and Bruneton's per-pattern artist λ, larger on long patterns, is the look this replaces with physics.
- Breaking foam does not persist. The threshold is solved for the coverage law's total coverage, which already contains maturing foam and streaks, and the instantaneous field shows it. Trails behind crests need the active and residual split, which waits on a cited active fraction.
- The coverage law applies down to its own offset (Brumer's 4.23 m/s, the wind below which no whitecaps are observed); at or below it the sea has no threshold and no foam. Above the fitted range the wind is held at 25.1 m/s.
- The threshold solve stays in `sim-ocean` and reads no render setting: it integrates the breaking-weighted divergence of the spectrum from the cut upward, which any valid cascade layout carries because the cutoff lies far above the floor tile. A quick-tier benchmark checks the tail generator's realised breaking-weighted divergence variance plus the band's against that integral within 5% on the three reference seas, and that a sea below the offset has no threshold.
- The Jacobian stays linearised (1 + ∇·D) and λ stays the scenario's: Dupuy and Bruneton's per-pattern determinant has a skewed tail with no closed-form solve.
- Breaking coverage is shaded at the lifetime reflectance at every distance, since foam age is not tracked.
- The foam buffer carries hull contact only. It stays indexed by undisplaced position, so foam rides the displacement exactly with no advection pass and no numerical diffusion (missing only the Stokes drift, a few tenths of a metre over a lifetime). Stamps arrive in world space and the stamp pass maps each to its undisplaced position by fixed-point iteration on the horizontal displacement of band plus displacing cascades, which converges wherever the surface is unfolded. Buffer foam decays at the cited time, is shaded from fresh to thin reflectance by density, and combines with whitecap coverage as 1 − (1 − W)(1 − d).
- Two step 1 departures are accepted: each tail bin's ω is rounded so the tail repeats every 1000 s, at most 0.3% phase speed error; and the drawn sea drops by d²/2R so the horizon dips below eye level, with the probe markers dropped alike.

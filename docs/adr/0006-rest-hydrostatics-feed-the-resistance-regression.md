# Rest hydrostatics feed the resistance regression; only the friction wetted area follows the attitude

A resistance regression reads the rest hydrostatics of the scenario's load condition: the canoe body's upright values (waterline length and beam, canoe draft, volume, waterplane and midship areas, centres of buoyancy and flotation, prismatic and midship coefficients, total draft, keel volume and keel centre-of-buoyancy depth) at the static equilibrium solved from the mass items with free trim, computed once at load and never again. The friction wetted area is the one input that follows the current heel, trim and sinkage from the per-tick hydrostatics pass. The hull chapter's alternative, recomputing every regression input from the mesh at the running attitude each tick, was rejected because the Delft series fits its heel effect as a separate term and its residuary already contains the free sinkage and pitch of the models, so running values would count heel and dynamic trim twice. The J-Class case that motivated it, a sailing length that grows with heel, does not arise for a hull inside a regression's family (the H-boat's heeled waterline shrinks) and belongs to the long-keel family that the chapter already expects to reformulate.

Decided by the ticket on resistance regression inputs ([#20](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/20)) on 2026-10-21, from the DSYHS research ([#6](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/6)) and the modern coefficient sources research ([#24](https://github.com/nipatiitti/luovi/issues/24)).

## Consequences

- A regression family block in the hydro block owns its whole convention as cited values: for `dsyhs`, the Reynolds length fraction 0.7, the hull form factor 1, the heel law constants, the appendage form factor law and the series parameter bounds. There is no generic form factor field, so a hull form factor can never be combined with a residuary that already carries form drag.
- The `dsyhs` block names the solid ids that form the keel and lists the appendage solids. Keel volume and centre-of-buoyancy depth are derived from the keel solids at load; an appendage bound to a lifting surface derives its chord and thickness ratio, one without carries them as estimates, and wetted area always comes from the solid.
- Inputs outside the fitted bounds are fed unclamped and reported once at load against the bounds in the block. Nothing clamps.
- Every Froude-indexed table carries its own rows, with a flagged zero row at Fn 0 added in the data package; evaluation is linear interpolation with the end row held above the last row. The residuary is evaluated on the magnitude of Fn and applied against the surge velocity, so sternway reads the same table and the code holds no range constants and no branch on direction.
- The trim correction of Keuning and Sonnenberg 1998 is left out until a benchmark miss asks for it.
- Water density and kinematic viscosity are scenario constants exposed by the environment query, not ship data and not per-point samples.
- The hull chapter's wording on per-tick regression hydrostatics is corrected through the design doc corrections ticket; the per-tick geometry pass stays for buoyancy and the friction wetted area.
